Send from **support@yahwehword.com**. Lockman's permissions form is at

**Subject:** Permission request — complete NASB 2020 text in two free,
non-commercial Bible apps

Dear Permissions Team,

I am writing to request permission to use the New American Standard Bible
(2020) in two free applications I develop and maintain. I understand that
what I am asking for goes beyond the Foundation's gratis-use provisions
for quotation, which is why I am writing rather than relying on them.

**Who we are.** We are [a non-profit Christian ministry / an independent
ministry project — fill in your actual status]. Both applications are
given away at no cost. There is no advertising, no subscription, no
in-app purchase, no paywall, and no sale or sharing of user data. Neither
project has any commercial revenue of any kind.

**1. Yahweh's Words** (yahwehword.com) — a Bible reader for Android, iOS,
Web, macOS, Windows and Linux, aimed principally at bilingual
Chinese-English readers. It offers several translations side by side; the
NASB is the English edition our readers most often pair with the Chinese
text. Our use requires the **complete text**, because the reader lets a
user open any chapter and read it offline.

**2. Yahweh's World** — a free bilingual (English / 简体中文) news reader
that pairs each news story with a single Bible verse and a short
reflection. It currently publishes about 72 stories a day across six
sections, each carrying one verse, with a rolling 90-day archive; so at
any moment roughly 6,500 individual verse quotations are live, refreshed
daily. We would like these quotations to be NASB rather than our own
rendering. This is quotation rather than a full text, but the cumulative
count is well past 1,000 verses and I would rather ask than assume.

**[disclosure]** Until this week the complete NASB text was also
downloadable as a single file from our website, as a side effect of how
the web build packages its data. That has been removed as of 2026-09-02
and the site now serves no bulk NASB file, pending your answer.

**What we would do.** We would display the copyright notice and the
attribution in the form you require, on the version-selection screen, in
the app's About page, and in the repository documentation. We would
follow whatever restrictions you set on caching, offline storage, export
and text selection. If the Foundation prefers, we are willing to
implement technical limits — for example disabling bulk copy or export of
NASB text.

**What we are asking.** Whether the Foundation is willing to grant a
licence for (1) bundling the complete NASB 2020 text within these free
applications for offline reading, and (2) the ongoing verse-by-verse
quotation described under Yahweh's World. If a full-text licence is not
available for a project of this kind, we would be grateful to know what
is — including whether a paid licence exists and on what terms, so that
we can decide accordingly. If the answer is no, we will remove the NASB
entirely and say so plainly to our readers.

Thank you for your time, and for the work of the Foundation.

With respect,

## Notes before sending

**The [disclosure] paragraph is a judgement call and it is yours.**
Including it is the honest course and it is also, I think, the safer one:
the file was publicly fetchable and archived by whoever chose to fetch it,
so the fact is discoverable, and a request that omits it reads badly if
they find out afterwards. It also demonstrates you acted before being
asked. Against it: it volunteers a compliance failure to the party
deciding your request. **My recommendation is to keep it** — it is two
sentences, it is already remediated, and it is what a person of good faith
would say. But delete it if you would rather, and the letter still stands
on its own.

**Do not describe this as "fair use" or lean on the free-quotation
provisions.** At 31,090 verses neither applies, and claiming they do
invites a boilerplate reply restating the 1,000-verse limit — which is
exactly the answer you already have.

**Ask for the full text explicitly.** The commonest way a request like
this gets answered uselessly is by being read as a quotation permission
request. The words "complete text", "bundled", and "offline" are doing
real work in the letter above; keep them.

**Set expectations.** Full-Bible digital redistribution is the case
publishers guard most closely. A free non-commercial ministry is a
genuinely favourable profile, but many publishers still require a formal
agreement and some charge. Plan for a no, and note that the app already
works without the NASB — KJV is public domain and unaffected.
