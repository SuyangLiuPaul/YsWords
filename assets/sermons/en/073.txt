# The Purpose of the Parables from the Salvation Viewpoint (Part 1)

Today we continue in our systematic exposition of God's word. And the last time I preached, you will recall that we expounded on the parable of the sower. Today we come to the next section, the section which is in fact wedged in, in Matthew chapter 13, into the parable of the sower. So, Matthew chapter 13, verses 10 to 17, the passage that we did not expound last time. Matthew chapter 13, verses 10 to 17. Now in this passage there are some very important things that the Lord wants to say to us. There are some very important questions and some important problems that we have to deal with. Which if we fail to deal with correctly, we are going to be very much on the wrong track in understanding God's word.

The parallel passages to this are in Mark chapter 4 and Luke chapter 8. But we shall not look at those because those are very much shorter than the Matthew passage, which is much longer and more full in its context. I shall read to you this passage, and I'd like you to bear in mind a number of questions as we read these.

The first question we want to ask is the question that comes up in this very passage. Why did the Lord Jesus teach in parables? Was it in order to conceal the truth? That is, to conceal the message of salvation to man? Or was it to reveal the message? That is the crucial point. What is the purpose of the parable? Is it to hide the message of salvation, or is it to reveal the message of salvation? If it hides the message to some, is that because that is God's intention to hide it? That is the kind of question we have to ask. Because this reveals and deals with the wider question: what is God's purpose towards us? Does he want to save us, or does he not want to save us? Now, if the purpose of a parable is to conceal salvation, and presumably the answer is that he does not want to save us — that seems a strange reply, but we shall see that in certain theologies that is in fact a reply. The theology of a Calvinistic type is in fact something of this sort. That it is designed to conceal rather than reveal. It is judgment rather than grace. We shall look at this in more detail. But these are the kind of questions I would like you to bear in mind as we go along. Why did the Lord Jesus speak to us in parables? Is salvation for everyone or not for everyone? That is the kind of question that we need to ask.

Now, as we bear these questions in mind, we read on. Matthew chapter 13, verse 10. "Then the disciples came and said to him" — that is, to Jesus — "Why do you speak to them in parables?" That is, why do you speak to the crowd, the multitude, in parables? "And he answered them, 'To you it has been given to know the secrets of the kingdom of heaven, but to them it has not been given.'" In regard to this sentence of the Lord Jesus, I want you to ask this question: Is this a statement of intention, or is this a statement of fact? I repeat the question. Is this a statement of intention, or is this a statement of fact? There is a crucial difference between this. Is it God's intention not to give the kingdom to some, and to give the kingdom to others? Which means, of course, the predestinarian teaching that some are to be saved, some are not to be saved. Or is it simply a statement of fact, not of intention? That in fact the kingdom of God has been given to you because you have received it. And in fact the kingdom of God has not been given to them because they have not received it. Which is the right answer? Very important. These are the kind of questions we must bear in mind.

To expound the word of God requires clear-minded logic, clear thinking, as well as accurate exposition. Any error, anywhere along the line, is going to have the most serious consequences. More serious than in any kind of study in this world you could pursue. An engineer who makes a structural design mistake will result in a bridge collapsing, something falling down, some lives being lost. But a teacher who makes a mistake may cost others their eternal well-being. An awesome responsibility that has never ceased to frighten me. But I proceed under the grace of God.

Verse 12: "For to him who has will more be given, and he will have abundance. But from him who has not, even what he has will be taken away. This is why I speak to them in parables." Why did he speak in parables? "This is why." What is "why"? Think about it. "This is why I speak to them in parables, because seeing, they do not see, and hearing, they do not hear, nor do they understand. With them indeed is fulfilled the prophecy of Isaiah which says: 'You shall indeed hear, but never understand, and you shall indeed see, but never perceive. For this people's heart'" — which people's heart? In the first place, of course, the people of Israel. The words were being addressed by Isaiah to the nation of Israel. "For this people's heart has grown dull, and their ears are heavy of hearing, and their eyes they have closed." Who have closed? They have closed. "Lest they should perceive with their eyes, and hear with their ears, and understand with their hearts, and turn to me to heal them." "But blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear. Now here you can clearly see the passage is not easy to understand. But precisely because it's not easy to understand, it contains very great truth in it. Great truth of great importance. Now here, as I said, we must bear in mind the question: why did the Lord Jesus preach in parables? He consistently, when he opened his mouth to the crowds, he spoke in parables. I return to this question: Was it in order to hide salvation from the people, or was it in order to reveal salvation? What is your answer to this question?

If you say it was designed to reveal the truth to them, then you have taken a position which is contrary to predestination and to Calvinism. If you say it is in order to conceal the truth from them, then you have taken the position which is represented by the predestination teaching. That God, in fact, spoke forth the word in such a way that it was a word not of salvation, but in fact of judgment. So that they could not see, could not understand. Salvation was there, but they could not perceive it. Only to those who were given the eyes to perceive, and given the ears to hear — only these, to whom God gave the capacity to understand, would understand. The others deliberately blinded.

Well, what's the evidence for this? The evidence for this is John chapter 12, verse 40. Put for us exceedingly plain. John chapter 12, verse 40. What does John chapter 12, verse 40 say? Quoting now from Isaiah with a different translation, we read this. In this teaching, the predestinarians rejoice. I do not rejoice. I do not glory in this teaching, because I am about to refute it. Now, if this be so, if this is correct, that God does not want you to be saved, then, my brothers and sisters, I shall close my Bible right here, and walk away from the pulpit, for I have no more message to preach. But before we rush into such conclusions, I praise God that the word of God is not to be understood quite so superficially. And this is what I aim to expound. Nowhere in the scripture is God's will ever made to become something which is unintelligible in the sense that there is no rhyme or reason to it. Nowhere do I see that. Then why do you use pictures? Why not speak plain language? The simple reason is that children cannot understand plain language. You've got to put it in pictures to help them to grasp it. And that's exactly what the Lord Jesus is doing. He comes to this multitude of people, most of them farmers, you well remember. What does he want to do? Is he going to give them an exposition as Paul does in Romans 8? Of course not. They can't understand it. What does he do? He speaks to them on their level. He tells them a story. He tells them a story for them to think about. And gradually, as they think about it, they get the message that is inside. Just as you sow the seed of a story in a child's heart, so that as the child grows and thinks about it — "Oh yeah, I begin to see now what was the point of that story." That is the whole purpose of the parable. God wants us to understand.

Now this takes us on to the question of Christians. Why then do Christians have such great problems understanding? Why? Well, because it is said here that their eyes they have shut. Their spiritual responses are dull. They do not open their eyes to the things of God. Paul says the same thing in 2 Corinthians 4, verse 3. He says, our gospel is not hidden. When Paul went out to preach the gospel, he did not preach so that they didn't understand. He preached with the intention that they should understand. And that is why he says in 2 Corinthians 4, verse 3, if our gospel is concealed or veiled or hidden, it is hidden only to those who are perishing. Why is it hidden to those who perish? Because they do not want to hear the truth. They do not want to respond to God's word.

Now here, in summary, contains an essence of truth we must grasp. You will understand only so much truth as you are willing to obey. I want to repeat this. It is the secret of the Christian life. It is the secret as to why some people are saved and some not saved, why some people become mighty spiritual giants and some people become spiritually nothing, spiritual dwarfs. And I repeat it again: you will only understand God's word in proportion as you are willing to obey it. By shutting the eyes is an act of will. They don't want to hear it. They don't want to see it.

I fear today that even if you are a Christian, you may have opened your eyes only in part. The rest you don't want to hear. You want only so much of the gospel as will be sufficient to get you to heaven. More than that, you don't want to hear. Am I right or am I wrong? I fear I touch you at the sore spot there. I suspect that many people go through great rallies, evangelistic rallies, because they only want so much of the gospel as will get them to heaven — no more and no less. And they hope that that will be the minimum, that the minimum will be required of them. Once that, that's enough. More than that, they don't want to hear any more than that.

Now if you think like this, you may end up with nothing at all. That's the significance of these words: "To him that has, will more be given. To him that has not, even what he has, he will lose." Or what he thinks he has, he will lose. You see, the point is this: you never know how much is just enough. When you come to the word of God, have you not often said, "Oh, I don't really like to listen to this. I don't think we have to practice this. This is far too demanding. This is not possible to practice." When the Lord Jesus says, "Except a man take up his cross and deny himself and follow me, he cannot be my disciple," what do you say? "Oh no, that's asking too much. Too much. Surely you can reduce the price of the gospel to something lower than that. Surely there must be the sale season right now. I mean, you have to come down with your prices. You've got to appeal to the crowd." The cost is too high. How do you expect me to be saved, become a disciple, if you want me to deny even myself? No way.

But the preacher comes along and says, "Now brothers and sisters, to be saved costs you nothing. Oh yes, all you do is believe in Jesus. Hallelujah! And you will be saved. And you will have peace, and you will have joy." See, I can do it pretty well too, right? Not bad. I can do that too. Then why don't I do it? Because I know I will not be speaking the truth. "It will cost you nothing. All you got to do is sign the decision card. But first you come forward while the choir is singing. They sing beautifully. Your heart is being moved. And while everybody is quiet, every head is bowed, you will come to the front. And thereafter a counselor will speak to you. All you got to do — that's all you got to do — believe in Jesus. That's all you got to do."

Oh, this is wonderful. Believe in Jesus. I am not even told what that means. What is there to worry about? There is no cost, nothing. I'm willing to listen to this. This I'm willing to listen to any time. Because it costs me absolutely nothing. And in fact, it is stated unashamedly: you have nothing to lose, only something to gain. As White Law put it in "The Reason Why," that very well publicized tract of which I'm utterly ashamed, I have to say. He is a businessman who once operated in Shanghai and wrote this tract which is distributed by the millions. And not surprisingly, of course, everybody is willing to have something for nothing. And so he goes around saying, "You see, becoming a Christian means you have everything to gain and you have absolutely nothing to lose." Now you read the Bible and you tell me where in the Bible you see this. "Except a man deny himself" — that's what Jesus said. But Mr. White Law and other people like this have other ways to present the gospel.

I don't intend to do this. I'm going to speak the truth. As I have said before, even if every single person walks out, if nobody ever comes to worship in this place again, it doesn't worry me. I have to speak the truth. That's my commission. If that truth blinds a person, it is not because I desire it to blind a person. It is because that's what the truth will do to those who don't want to hear it.

My brothers and sisters, I ask you to judge for yourself whether this is the gospel which we preach, whether this is what God is saying to us. He is not willing that any should perish. He paid the price of our redemption. But he wants a total response from us. He gives himself completely to us. The least he requires from us is that we give ourselves totally to him. That is what belief in the biblical sense means. Not just that Jesus died for me, but that because he died for me, I believe with the total response of saying from now on what the apostle Paul said: "I live no more for myself, but for him who died for me and rose again."

Paul says in 2 Corinthians 5, nobody ever lives to himself again who is a Christian. He lives only for him, that is, for Jesus who died for him. That is the gospel. And anything else that is preached that says all you have to do is believe that Jesus died for you — that's not all. That is an important, essential part of it, but not all. If you believe that Jesus died for you, if you really believe it, what should be your response? Even if you didn't know the Bible, what should be your response? For me, I can see the only response is that if he gave himself for me, I give myself for him. That's the only response of faith possible. Where else can we lower the price of salvation and dilute and adulterate the gospel?

And so my question to you is this. You say you believe in Jesus? Praise God. But do you understand that it means living for him? And again, I am not talking about full-time service and the like. I mean living for him wherever you are right now — as a student, as an office worker, in the factory, wherever you are. Living for Jesus where you are. Doing that work for him, that job for him, studying for him. You belong to him. As Paul says, you were bought with a price. You belong to him. That's why the one title that Paul gloried in is the slave of Jesus Christ. The word servant, as you all know, means slave. Every letter that Paul opens, he says, "I belong to Jesus." He declared, "I have given myself to him. He has bought me. I am his. I am a slave of Jesus Christ. I rejoice to be his slave." Do you rejoice? That is salvation.

He is not willing that any should perish. He speaks to us the truth as plainly as we can understand it. And I have sought to speak to you the truth as plainly as you can understand it. And blessed are you — in verse 16 — "Blessed are your eyes, for they see, and your ears, for they hear." Blessed are your eyes. Blessed are you. Who are the "you"? Who? Who is he speaking to? He is speaking to his disciples. Whose eyes are blessed because they see? The disciples. Why? Because they have yielded themselves totally to him. No wonder they are blessed. No wonder they are blessed.

If today you have already committed yourself fully to Christ as the disciples did, then indeed blessed are you. Because now your eyes see. Has it ever struck you how clearly your eyes can see? Do you not praise God? Do you not praise God how clearly your ears can hear? Do you not rejoice in the fact that God has caused the Holy Spirit to fill your heart? As it says in Romans chapter 5, verse 5, he has shed abroad God's love into our hearts by his Spirit. And therefore, the secret — if it is a secret at all — is simply in these words. The whole difference, I say and say once more, whether God's word is open to you or closed to you, is not because God wants you not to understand. God forbid such teaching. God wants you to understand. God wants you to understand. He puts it to you as clearly as it is ever possible to put it. If necessary by illustration, which is what the Lord Jesus does here, by parable. Whether you understand or not depends on you. Yes.
