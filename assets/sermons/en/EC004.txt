# Freedom in Christ (Side A)

I will read to you the words in Luke chapter 4, which the Lord Jesus read to the congregation in the synagogue, which sums up the nature of His ministry: "The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because He has anointed me to bring good news to the poor. He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives, and recovery of sight to the blind, to let the oppressed go free, to proclaim the year of the Lord's favor."

Now in this very short few lines, something is repeated twice, which in Scripture means we need to pay attention, because Scripture does not repeat itself for no other purpose. He says, "He has sent me to proclaim release to the captives." And then another line, two down, it says, "to let the oppressed go free." The meaning of these two statements are, of course, identical. The idea is to release captives, to set people free.

One of the most fearful things is addiction. We are more familiar with drug addiction, but there are all kinds of addictions. And I can say, without fear of contradiction, that everyone is under some form of addiction. You may not be under addiction to heroin, or cocaine, or alcohol, or whatever it is. But everyone is under addiction, or bondage of some sort. A great tragedy is often that we are blind to our own situation. And that is why in the middle of these two statements about being set free, or freed from oppression, you have the statement, "recovery of sight to the blind." Of course, in this context, the reference is not as though the Lord is concerned only with healing the physically blind. No, the reference here is to the spiritual. The problem is we are under bondage without knowing that we are under bondage.

The frightening thing about addiction, it seems, is a capturing of the will. We start out with a little bit of alcohol, or whatever it is you take. Now, some people can take alcohol, like the French do in France, and they drink it every day, without becoming addicted to it. There are others who cannot do that. In other words, some things are addictive to some people, but not addictive to others. And in part, it depends for what reason you use that substance.

Now, there are other kinds of bondage. I think without fear of contradiction, I can say that every human being is in bondage to himself or herself, in bondage to self-interest, in other words, to selfishness. Every person is selfish. Until God does something with that person, he is always addicted to selfishness. The ego, the self, is always central. What that does in a society is not hard to understand. When everybody goes for themselves, nobody cares for the other. At least not, if that person's interest has no bearing on my own. I care for somebody else only if that person's interest happens to affect me. Then, of course, I care, because that person's interest is my own interest. That is where the root of the problem of human society is.

Jesus came to produce a new kind of society in which the command to love the neighbor as oneself is meant to be implemented. But it is impossible until we are changed, until we are transformed. And that's why the gospel is about change and transformation. But it can't be done until we are liberated, until we are freed. Those of us who come from China are very used to the word "liberation." It's been thrown around all over the place. We have a liberation army. We have all kinds of liberation. In other words, the gospel has to do with freedom. And you will remember that in the camp I used Romans 6 and verse 7: "He who has died with Christ is freed." Freed from sin.
