Did he spit on the Queen? No. He only spat on the image. But you don't have to be a genius to understand that the action has indicated something very serious. You have insulted the Queen. You have stepped on her image. But you say, "I didn't step on the Queen." Right, you didn't. But you did step on the image. And what is intended by this is clear.

Now try to grasp the significance, and you are beginning to think in a new way. If every man is an image of God, and you say to him, "You fool, you idiot, raca" — raca, maybe we don't know the precise meaning of the word, it doesn't matter, the intention is perfectly clear — the Lord says you will be guilty of hellfire. You say, "Surely this is exaggerating. Lord, you're always exaggerating, aren't you?" Oh, he is not exaggerating. Because that sentence has meaning only when you understand that man is in the image of God. You have insulted God by insulting his image, just as you would spit on the image of the Queen or step on her image.

What is the coin? What is the flag? The flag is just a piece of cloth with some colors on it. You try spitting on a flag in front of the people. You try stepping on that flag and you'll be torn apart. I mean, you'll be thrown in jail. You'll say, "Come on, I only stepped on a piece of cloth, and not a very clean piece of cloth at that." You see, man understands the significance of image when it comes to worldly things, right? But he doesn't understand the significance when it comes to spiritual things. That is why if you insult your brother, the Lord said, you are guilty of hellfire. You have insulted his image. But why do we have no problem there before? Because we never thought of man as seriously being in the image of God. And a Christian is doubly in the image of God, both by the old covenant and the new covenant.

The change of thinking, the way we look at people, has to be radically changed. But now we understand what John is saying in 1 John chapter 4, verse 20, when we understand this logical premise. John is saying this: if you do not love the brother whom you can see as being the image of God, how would you love him whom that image represents? Oh, I never thought of it like that. Exactly. That's where our problem is. We never thought of it like that. We thought brother is brother. I mean, he's nothing like God, he's not remotely like him. But God says he is the image. He is the image. Whether we like it or not, he's the image.

That coin may be rather soiled. It may be even a bit rusty. It may be distorted, it may be scratched. But you step on it and you have stepped on the image of the Queen. Soiled or not soiled, rusty or not rusty, scratched or not scratched, it is still the image of the Queen. A flag — it may be dirty, it may be torn, it may have been captured by the enemy, whatever, but any insult against that flag is an insult against the image of that country. Everybody knows it. That's why if you want to insult the United States, you burn the United States flag, because it's the image of the United States. You can do the same thing with a coin, you can do the same thing with a stamp, a postal stamp, anything, because these are images of that country or of the sovereign of that country or the leader of that country.

When we arrived in Florida, I saw there was a picture of President Reagan. What is a photograph? A photograph is nothing but a piece of paper hanging there on the wall. If you try spitting on that photograph, you'll see what happens. I mean, you'll probably be beaten into the ground and carried away by the police, probably carried away in an ambulance. You say, "Come on, be reasonable, I only spat on a piece of paper." Oh no, it's not a piece of paper. It's an image. That's what you spat on, not a piece of paper.

You and I may only be a piece of paper, worth nothing, as far as the world is concerned, worth nothing, worth a few cents. I mean, a one-cent coin is worth one cent. You step on that one-cent coin in front of anyone who belongs to the Commonwealth or to Britain and you'll see what happens to you. And rightly so, because you have insulted what is precious to them, even though that coin is worth only one cent. You and I may be worth only one cent, right? Maybe less than one cent in our eyes, or in other people's eyes. But you are incredibly precious in one sense: you bear his image. And anyone who insults you insults God, because you bear his image. And you may be worth one cent, but woe betide anyone who dares to insult you, call you idiot, fool, raca. Hellfire awaits that person. Is that my teaching? That is what the Lord says there in the Sermon on the Mount, not me. And that logic we could never understand until this day because we have not understood the teaching of image.

Oh, brothers and sisters, we've got to change the way we look. Now, if man is the image of God, it follows that any service I do for the sake of that image, I do for the sake of him who has given that image, of his own grace and of his choice. I may not like my brother or my sister. I may not like that coin. I don't like its color. I don't like its shape. I don't like it that it's dirty. I don't like that it's scratched. But you are going to have to argue with him, not with that coin. That's what you're going to have to argue with.

Now, you see that we previously posited that we were talking about two directions — loving God with all our hearts, loving the brother — and this is impossible, precisely because we thought they were two entirely different directions, but they are not. Now you realize we are talking about the same direction, that any action performed for or against the brother or the sister has been performed for or against God. And that is why the Lord Jesus says, "He that receives you receives me. He that persecutes you persecutes me." You are me. You are my image. You are my representative. And whether the world likes you as my representative or not, whether other brothers and sisters like you as my representative or not, is not the point. I choose to have you as my representative.

Can you grasp this way of thinking? Difficult, isn't it? We have never thought quite like that before. Now we see that the second commandment is like the first commandment precisely because the second is the image of the first. And what you do to the one is what you do to the other. How you react to the one is how you react to the other.

Now, the Lord in his wisdom has done marvelous things. How wise God is. How he catches us in our hypocrisy and in our superficiality. What do I mean? You and I can easily profess, "Oh, I love God so much. I love him, I adore him, I praise him." And he says, "Really? Prove it. Do what you would do for me to the image that I have appointed."

Do you see what I'm getting at? Do you see what God's wisdom is? Precisely because he doesn't have a need, it's easy to love him.
