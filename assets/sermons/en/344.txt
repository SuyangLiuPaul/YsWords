We continue our study today in the Word of God. In our series on regeneration and renewal, we have been speaking last Sunday about the fact that perfection, biblical perfection, is the goal of renewal. That is the direction of the Christian life. Where are we heading for in the Christian life? What is the purpose of this being renewed by the Spirit in our mind? What Paul speaks about, the renewal of your mind. Where is the goal that we are aiming at? And we saw it is -- perfection means to be like Christ, to become like Him.

And so let us sum up a few points that we talked about last week. We saw that when we speak of becoming like Christ, what exactly do we mean? Do we mean that we should all become gentle and meek and mild? Does it mean simply that we are talking about a certain type of character that we can picture in our mind? We shall see that there are aspects to this where Jesus is not necessarily meek and mild. There is always the danger, as Albert Schweitzer long ago pointed out in his work, Quest of the Historical Jesus, the great danger that we fashion Christ in our own image, rather than, as the scripture teaches, that we are fashioned by Christ in His image. We make a Christ in our own image. We imagine Him to be meek and mild, which sometimes He is. But we must be careful that we do not define holiness in terms of merely human virtues. God's character is not like our character, nor His virtues entirely our virtues.

When the Lord Jesus drove the merchants out of the temple with a whip of cords, He was not exactly meek and mild. And our concept of His character begins to be disturbed, because He doesn't fit into our scheme of human virtues. When He overturned the tables of the money changers, we feel a little offended, because our definition of holiness is not necessarily God's definition of it. So, when we say that we become like Jesus, we must become like the Jesus that is manifested in the scriptures, and not a Jesus of our own making. But much more, we saw that in order to keep our definition of holiness on trust, that to become like Jesus means not just becoming like Him in what we think His character was, but much more, that we become like Him in fulfilling the same mission that He fulfilled. That is what discipleship is about. He walks in front, we follow after. As Peter puts it, we walk in His footsteps, which very few Christians are doing today, needless to say.

Very few Christians are doing today. We do not walk in His footsteps. We do not walk where He walked. We do not walk in the direction that He went. Renewal, then, is to become like Him. But to become like Him is to do, first and foremost, what He did. All this we saw last week from John chapter 14, verse 12. "He who believes in Me, the works that I do, he will do also." And we saw that this does not apply primarily, even less solely, to miracles. We saw that the work of Jesus refers to His entire mission that God entrusted to His hands. And the Lord Jesus says, "He who believes in Me" -- any ordinary Christian, according to the biblical definition of Christian, which again is not necessarily the same as the way the churches today define Christian -- but in the biblical definition, or in the Lord's teaching of a believer, "He who believes in Me, will do" -- not probably will, maybe will, or ought to do, but will do the works that I do. In other words, in the Lord's teaching, if you believe in Him, if you truly believe in Him, you will do the work that He did. You will engage in the same saving mission in which He was engaged.
