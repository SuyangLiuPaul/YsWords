And I think that's precisely why — because the carnal man is terrified of God. He doesn't even dare to know what the will of God is.

But if you don't want to know his will, you don't dare to know his will, don't come to me and tell me that God is not real. Because the moment you are committed to do his will, he sure will tell you what the truth of the matter is. That's the words of the Lord Jesus himself in John chapter 7, verse 17: "If any man's will is to do his will, he will know of the truth." He will know whether what I say is true or not. It will be inescapable.

I hope you did not come to church today just to have been able to say, "Well, it's Christmas today, so he has been in church." I want you to be on this Christmas day faced with the reality of these words — whether you are willing to say on this Christmas day, whether you are a Christian or a non-Christian, whichever way: "I have come, O God, to do your will." I came to this church — maybe I didn't know what I came for in the first place, maybe I came because it's Christmas day — but now before I leave, I am willing to look at this challenge. I am willing, O God, to do your will, whatever it is. You will be on the way to eternal life. You are going to live in the new quality of life.

And if this were not true, if I didn't speak from my own experience, if I were talking through my hat — I am sure you can discern whether I speak the truth or not. He is the living God. You just say it quietly in your heart to him: "God, I don't know you now, and the reason I don't know you is because I haven't been doing your will. I haven't been wanting to do your will. But now I come, O God, to do your will, whatever that is." You will know him. You will know whether he is the living God.

And there are Christians here today who don't know the living God. They are professing Christians. They got baptized, they went to church, they did this and that, but God is not real to them. Why? Because they are still struggling with the question of whether they are going to do his will or not, whether they will do his will fully or not. There is only one way to do God's will: you either totally commit to doing that will, or you don't do his will. Because no partial doing of his will is going to be acceptable to him. Don't say, "Well, I did this part of his will, I did that part of the will." You are going to do his will as a whole, or you don't do it at all. God does not settle for half measures.

I speak as one by the grace of God, unworthy as I am, who know this to be true. And I wish that every Christian would know on this Christmas day what it means in your own experience — not just to read it here in the Bible, because the Bible is meant to be turned into experience, something that you know to be true through your own life. I know this to be true because I lived it, and by the grace of God I am still living it. "I have come, O God, to do your will." Any such person who lives according to these words of Christmas — why Jesus came — will know the living God. I guarantee it, because the Word of God guarantees it.

But one last point, and we must close as our time is pretty nearly gone, and it is this. Someone will say, "Yes, well, it is okay for Jesus to do the will of his Father, isn't it, because he knows the Father. I mean, after all, the first chapter of John tells us he was face to face with the Father. If I had this face-to-face experience with the Father, I am most probably doing his will too." You would have to say probably, I dare not — you would not dare to say, "I am sure." I certainly will. The Lord Jesus knew him face to face. He talks with the Father face to face. You know him that well, there is a very high probability that you will do his will with that kind of intensity, isn't it?

And maybe you are saying to yourself, "Yeah, well, you see, my problem is, it is okay for you. You stand there saying, you know, you concentrate and all this about doing the will of the Father. I mean, you have been walking with the Lord 20, 30 years." No, not quite 30 years. Thirty years — I am giving my age away. Must be coming close to 30 years, I have to confess. "That's okay, so you know the Lord perhaps at a level that we don't know. So it is okay, you are very concentrated." It is not that way. It is not that way, because I did not know him at the beginning either. We do not know the Father. We do not know God.

That is amazing. I was meditating the other day, and the more I meditated on this point, the more surprised I was. I came to one remarkable conclusion, that actually I know more about the history of God's dealings with men, I know more about God's character and God's ways through his dealings with men, than I even know about the life, the biography, the history of my wife. Amazing. And the more I thought about it, it is true. I know more about God and his dealings and his character than I know even about my wife, the closest person to me.

As I look back and I thought about it, I knew next to nothing about the first 20 years of my wife's life. Well, I know she went to a certain school. I know — I met her parents, I met a few of her friends, I know where she lived in Hong Kong. These things I know. But I know relatively — if I put everything I know about my wife, the first 20 years of her life — she's still very young, of course, but the first 20 years of her life, because when I first met her, not that long ago, she was only around 20 then, you see. And I look back, and if I put down everything I know about my wife's life and biography, I wonder if I could manage to fill even one sheet of ordinary letter paper. But I know about God in such detail and immensity. Why? Look at this book, the Bible. I know God in all his dealings with this person and that person, and with this nation and with that nation, what he did and what he said. I don't know a single thing that my wife ever said in the first 20 years of her life. I don't have a quote on it. But look at what the Lord said — I have volumes and volumes, pages and pages of what the Lord said, what he taught the Israelites, what he commanded them to do, where he led them to go, the things that he did. I know volumes and volumes of things that he did. And yet the person closest to me, I don't know half as well.

In fact, I realized that I could predict the Lord's doings and actions far more precisely than I could predict what my wife would do in a given situation. I could guess — she would most probably do this — but I couldn't say for certain, because she might forget, as most people often do forget. She might forget this or forget that, or she might just happen not to do what I expect her to do at that time. But God, because he's the same yesterday, today, and forever — when you know him well, you could predict his actions with astonishing accuracy.

My wife herself is a witness to that. As I shared — one striking example was when I predicted the exact timetable of what the Lord would do in a given situation. I said to her, "In three days' time the Lord is going to do this," and I told her the whole timetable: "He's going to do this, and then the next day he's going to do this, and the next day he's going to do that." And I said, "I'm telling you this in advance. You're going to be my witness, because if I tell you afterwards and I said, 'You see, I thought so,' there's nothing there. But if I tell you three days in advance what he's going to do and that timetable, you will be my witness whether this is true." And she is my witness — it came to pass exactly as I said it would, on the third day, on the fourth day, on the fifth day, exactly as I predicted he would do it, he did it.

So how is it possible for you to know? I know. That's why we have the Bible in front of us. I know exactly how his character is. I know how his mind works — I mean "exactly" in so far as, by the Lord's grace, I'm able to discern it from Scripture. It's an amazing thing to realize that I know God better than anyone else in this world, and you can too. Amazing. And it struck me the other day, and I was filled with joy as I paced up and down in the kitchen. I said, "Amazing — the closest person to me in this world, I don't know as well as I know God." I could not predict what my wife would do with that kind of accuracy. That's sure, she will understand, and you will understand — you can't predict anyone's actions with that accuracy. But because God is unchanging in his inner character, and because the whole Bible has revealed him to me in such detail, if we only bother to study the Scripture with that accuracy, you would know God in so intimate and so precise a way that you couldn't believe that there's anyone in this world that you know so well as God.

And so let's look at the picture again. You say, "Well, if I knew God, I would do his will." You don't know God? What do you got a Bible for? What is the Bible doing in front of you? It's there telling you what he is like. It even quotes his words. It tells you how he acts in this way, how he reacts in that way, what he does in this situation, what he does in that situation. And you still don't know what God is like? And I wonder what you've been reading.

Isn't that wonderful? Isn't that exciting, that there is one person in this world — and the one person that really matters — who has revealed himself completely to us? Jesus, we learn, came into the world, in John chapter 1, verse 18, he came into the world in order to do what? To reveal the Father to us. He has revealed the Father to us. If the rest of the Old Testament was not enough, then he has revealed himself to us completely in Jesus. Now what more can you ask for? Of course, if you want to experience him, the only way to do that is to be committed to do his will. Only when you are unconditionally committed to do his will, all these things you read about will become part of your experience. Otherwise they will just be some knowledge that you don't even know what to do with.

May it please the Lord then that God accomplish his purpose in each one of your lives at this Christmas and every other Christmas. I hope you will remember these words again: "I have come, O God, to do your will." Why did he give us a new life? So we can twiddle our fingers and wait till we get to heaven, and then when we get to heaven we don't know what to do there? There is a whole program for us. But we must first be committed unconditionally and without reserve to do his will, and then you will experience his power in your life, and then you are going to know him as the living God. You will have the gift of life, and through that gift, the experience of the living God. Let us pray.
