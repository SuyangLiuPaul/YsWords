But I find how so much of false teaching is often so vague, that you are not sure exactly what it has said. In the outside Shanghai, I often like to relate this, there is a temple. The example that comes to my mind is that particular statement: "Parents both, you cannot lose one." I always remember that statement. Now the beauty of this statement is that whatever happens to your parents is always right. It can mean, "Parents both, you cannot lose one," meaning your parents will be both together, and you will not lose one. But also it can say, depending on the punctuation, "Parents both cannot, you will lose one." That means the two cannot be both, one will be lost. You see, you just put the punctuation in. You see, you read it the other way, it should be "Parents both cannot." But if you lose both, it says, "Parents both, you cannot lose one," means that if you lose one, you lose both. You see, you cannot lose only one. If you lose one, you lose both.

So in either way, if you lose both your parents, you lose one parent, or you don't lose any parent, the statement is always right. That is why, you see, there is also not only in the Chinese side, there was also in the Greek, in every type of fortune telling, there is this kind of element of deceit in it.

Some of you will know the actual factual case of history, where the king, the famous king of Croesus, went to see a fortune teller whether he should cross the river and attack the Persian Empire.
