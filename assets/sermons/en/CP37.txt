To say that I believe in Jesus that he died for me is something talking about an event 2,000 years ago. Does Jesus do nothing today? He only did something 2,000 years ago? Does he still do anything today? Yes, he does. What does he do? He directs my life every moment of every day. Can we dare to say that? Your Jesus is really alive then. Some people believe in a dead Jesus who passed away a long time ago. If we do nothing, then he does everything. If he does everything, then he is my life. That's what Philippians 1:21 means.

John chapter 5, verse 30. Let me read this to you too. "I can of myself do nothing." Here it is again. "Of myself I can do nothing." Surely he can. Of course he can. But he will not. "I cannot do it" does not mean that he is not able to do it. He has chosen not to do it. The "cannot" is a cannot of will. A cannot of choice. "I can of myself do nothing. As I hear I judge, and my judgment is righteous, because I do not seek my own will but the will of the Father who sent me." To do nothing here is defined very precisely as doing nothing of one's own will. Very clearly. It is the Father's will that he does.

Verse 19, a few verses earlier: "Then Jesus answered and said to them, 'Most assuredly I say to you, the Son can do nothing of Himself, but what He sees the Father do. For whatever He does, the Son also does in like manner.'" Whatever the Father does, the Son also does. That is what it means for me to live is God. God is my life. He defines my life. I can do nothing. I want to do nothing but his will. Can you say that honestly? Is that the kind of Christian life that you live? Or you hope to be saved by some superficial pledge of believing that Jesus died for you? That kind of Christianity doesn't exist in the New Testament. Is this too radical for you? If Jesus is my life, then this is the way I live, or else he is not my life and he is not your life. And if he is not your life, please tell me how you hope to be saved. We are talking about life. Life is salvation. Salvation is life. This is the only kind of salvation. This is the only kind of life I can see in the New Testament.

Let's go on quickly to the fourth. And the fourth point is this: I know nothing. I don't know anything either. Well, it's going from one nothing to another. The Christian life is to know nothing. Let me read to you 1 Corinthians 2, verse 2. Here is the guiding principle of Paul's life. That is the secret by which he lives. What does he know? "For I determined not to know anything among you except Jesus Christ and him crucified."

And then he goes on to say, yes, he has learned the philosophy of this world. Paul was a very learned man. He was so learned that in one of the trials he was placed on, his judge said to him, "Paul, you are mad because of your great learning. You know too much. You are simply too learned and your learning has made you mad." Paul said, "No, I am not mad. I know very clearly what I am talking about." But why did they think he was mad? Look at this. For him, the only knowledge from now on, ever since he came to the Lord, the only thing that mattered to him was Jesus Christ and him crucified. Jesus Christ — one can still handle — and him crucified. A crucified Christ. A dead man? What is this? He is really mad. Ah, he goes on to say, yes, but that is the power of God to salvation. What? A crucified Christ? He goes on to say that is the power of God.

That doesn't mean that you abandon your secular knowledge, that you were trained in computers and then you suddenly went back and said, "I don't know anything about computers anymore." No. What it means is that that kind of knowledge no longer determines your living. Before, that was everything. Your skill, your knowledge, you were proud of it. You considered yourself to be something. Now, your functional principle is none of this. For you, the functional principle of your life, that which determines your knowledge and everything else, is subject to this: Jesus Christ is the center and key. And this Jesus Christ is not seen primarily as king of the universe. He is that. But that is not the point of the New Testament. It is Jesus Christ lowly and meek. That is what "crucified" means. How lowly and how meek? Remember, we are talking about Philippians. In chapter 2, he goes on to say, "He was equal with God, king of kings, Lord of lords. But what did he do? He humbled himself down to the earth." At Christmas time, we talk about him being born in a manger. That's not the lowest he went. He went a lot lower than that. He died as a criminal on the cross. That is how low he went. It is this Jesus — not the king of kings with the crown on his head, multiple crowns on his head — that is not the center of Paul's knowledge, amazingly. What is? The one with no crown on his head except the crown of thorns. Can you understand this? It is easy to glory in the Lord who reigns over the universe. That is not the Lord that Paul was glorying in. He gloried in a God who got crucified on earth. He gloried in a God who humbled himself to become a slave.

Now notice what happens when this kind of thinking dominates your thought. You become a Christian of a different quality. Not the power Christian. Not the glorious Christian that boasts about God as king of kings, although he is that too. But boasts in a God who made himself lower than a slave. For Paul who lives with this kind of thinking as determining his entire thought process, it gives a kind of quality, a kind of humility, a kind of graciousness, a kind of lowliness about a person's life that is indefinable. I am still trying to learn that. I meditate every day on the cross of Christ. I am not meditating about Jesus as king of kings. That is easy to understand. What is so hard for me to understand is Jesus, the one who was lower than the slave, who washed his disciples' feet and then went on to die for his disciples and for all of us who have become his disciples, and who forever will walk about in heaven with the nail prints in his hands, the scars on his brow, and the stab wounds in his heart. It's this Jesus that I am trying to understand, and I am very slow of understanding, but I am trying. And Paul says, that is the Jesus that I want to know. To know him is to understand all mysteries. Everything else I don't even want to know.

The fifth point — because there are many other references but we are out of time, I will move on. The fifth point: when all the foregoing four points are true, the fifth point is: I fear nothing. Ah, now the strength of the spiritual man begins to appear. I fear nothing. This is a striking thing, and here again I have too many references. I'll read just one or two verses. Philippians 1:28 will suffice for the moment. Here we are back in Philippians 1 again. Philippians 1:28: "And in nothing be terrified by your adversaries." The man who functions in this way is a fearless man.

You know, in my teenage days I ran a ball team. A baseball team. And you know what I chose for the name of that team? It was called "Dreadnought." Dreadnought means to fear nothing. "Dread" is fear, "nought" is nothing. Dreadnought. Because I was very interested in military science and you know that there's a famous battleship called the Dreadnought. It's said to be unsinkable. Fear nothing. And so in Chinese our team was called "Afraid of nothing." The true Christian is afraid of nothing for one simple reason: because the only one that he fears is God. When the Lord Jesus was in the storm, the ship was sinking. Did you see any fear in him? He was sound asleep. Fearless. There you see the quality: fear nothing.

You know that John Wesley was so impressed by a bunch of Christians on the boat going over to North America, because at that time he was a Christian but he was an unregenerate Christian like so many. And he saw these Christians — the Pietists, they were called by the Germans — they had such trust. To believe in the Lord Jesus Christ means you trust your whole life unconditionally, totally, into his hands so that you have no fear. Belief is taken to the level where fear is written off. Is that your belief in Jesus? Or does your exam make you anxious? Or does your health make you anxious? Or does your marriage or forthcoming marriage make you anxious? Everything makes you anxious. The frightened, anxious Christian. If you live like that, you don't know what it means. To live is Christ means you have no fear because he lives in you. The responsibility is on him.

I can speak of this from my own experience. I lived for nearly three years in China in a state of abject poverty — not a cent. I lost everything with the coming of the communists. Every day I had to trust the Lord to supply my needs. I would get up in the morning and I have not the faintest idea where I would get the next meal. Imagine that — penniless and trying to live nearly three years like that. If you had to rise this morning with not a cent in your pocket, with no lunch ahead of you and no supper, and no supper tomorrow and all the next coming weeks and months, what would your condition be? You would be relaxed, you would be full of peace and joy, only if Christ is your life. I would get up in the morning and I said, "Lord, here is your child. Lord, I'm hungry. I've got nothing to eat. I've also got no money to buy anything with. I'm not fearful. I know that somehow you're going to provide my need for today." Did he ever fail? Never. Why do I preach the gospel with conviction? Because the God who fed those two million people in the wilderness with manna from heaven is the God I know, who fed me every day, and I didn't starve either. That's how practical my God is. Somebody asked me, "Can God feed you when you're hungry?" Sure he can. The God who created heaven and earth cannot make enough food to feed you? No fear.

The sixth point is this: I lack nothing. Whether physical or spiritual, the person who lives in Christ has everything. That is what he said: "As having nothing, and yet possessing all things." He lacks nothing. Let me read just one verse. 1 Thessalonians chapter 4, verse 12: "That you may walk properly toward those who are outside and that you may lack nothing." Live in such a way that you lack nothing. Now this lacking of nothing can be the Lord's provision directly, or if circumstances permit, then you can also work with your own hands as the apostle Paul did and earn what you need. When there was opportunity for me to earn my own income, I was not so dependent upon the Lord that I would say, "Oh, you can provide me with food so I don't have to work anymore." No. I went out to work. When I first took up the pastorate at the church in Liverpool, the church was so small — I had only a handful of people. They couldn't support me. And I said, "That's alright with me. I'll go out to work." And so I went out to teach. So I taught from Monday to Friday and then I worked in the church from Saturday and Sunday, and on Monday I went back to work. But the Lord in his kindness, after six months, the church grew to sufficient growth where they told me that I needn't continue teaching anymore, that they would from now on be able to give me the same amount that I was receiving through teaching. But whether the Lord provides directly or through our own working, more importantly, we lack nothing on the spiritual level. How is your condition spiritually? Do you feel the emptiness inside? Do you feel that every day you seem to be lacking something in your life and you don't know what that lack is? The answer is quite simple. What you lack is precisely this same theme. You have not yet implemented the truth that Christ is your life. As long as Christ is not your life, believe me, you can try as hard as you like to fill the emptiness in your life — it will never work. You will always be haunted by that nagging emptiness. And after today's message, don't ever say you didn't know how to fill the emptiness. I have told you what the scriptural teaching is. There is no easy way, there is no cheap way, but there is a wonderful way. Christ will fill your life to overflowing, and you will lack nothing at all.

Let me move on to the last point now. The seventh: because you lack nothing, you also need to withhold nothing. There is another nothingness in the New Testament teaching: to withhold nothing. You have so much to give that you don't have to withhold anything. Have you ever met this kind of Christian that seems to have limitless resources? They always have something to give. They are so rich they don't have to withhold anything. They don't have to be stingy. Let me read just one reference: Acts 20, verse 20. "And now I have kept back nothing that was helpful, but proclaimed it to you and taught you publicly from house to house." I have kept back nothing. There is this seventh nothing. Have you kept back anything from the Lord? Have you in fact anything to give to the Lord or to each other? Is yours a life of richness? Is yours a life of power?

What kind of a Christian life, let me ask you in closing, do you want to live? Do you just want to be an ordinary, average, not-so-good, not-so-bad Christian? Or do you want to make your life count in this generation, through whom God can work? Through whom God can shake not only Singapore but the world? God is looking for that kind of person. Paul was so abundant that he says, "It is a joy for me to be spent for you." That was the mentality with which he worked. 2 Corinthians 12:15: "I will gladly give you everything, to be spent for you." In Romans chapter 9, verse 3, he says, "I am willing even" — this is to what extent he is willing to withhold nothing. When he says "nothing," he literally means nothing. "I will withhold nothing, not even my salvation." What did you say? Yes, not even his salvation. Romans 9:3. Many people say, "I'll give you everything except my salvation. No, no, that's for me." Paul says no, not even my salvation is precious to me. If through my being lost you can be saved, so be it. Romans 9:3 often brings tears to my eyes, where Paul says if through his being lost, his people the Jews can be saved, he would gladly offer up his salvation for their eternal welfare. What is Paul saying? He is saying that he is willing to go to hell if the Jewish people, his own people, can go to heaven. How many of you dare to say here you are willing to go to hell if the people of Singapore can go to heaven? How many here would dare to say you are willing to go to hell if the people of China can hear the gospel and be saved? Where can God find such a man? But all this is part of the meaning "to live is Christ." It is not some kind of super-Christianity, though to our ears it sounds like super-Christianity, isn't it? But that's simply part of the definition of what it means for me to live is Christ.

Our time is gone. Let us close in prayer.
