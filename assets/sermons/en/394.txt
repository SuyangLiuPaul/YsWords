# Love (Part 1)

When I was a teenager, in my early teens, I often stayed in the home of a couple. This couple had no children of their own, and so they treated me as their own child whenever I stayed with them. They were very kind, very loving to me, and I often stayed with them, especially during school holidays, because my school was very near their home, whereas my own home was quite far away at the time.

Now this couple were extremely wealthy. They had a beautiful house. They had a very successful business. And they had really everything that the world was able to offer. But I noticed that though they had everything, in fact they had nothing. Now what do I mean by that? What I mean is that they had all the material possessions you could offer. They had far too much. You know, they had so much money that often they didn't know what to do with this money. So they would buy lots of things that were no use to them and no use to anyone.

For example, they would buy a lot of very expensive Persian carpets or Chinese carpets. Now you know that one carpet of high quality could cost thousands of dollars, and it was very beautiful. The problem is that when you buy that many carpets, there's nowhere to put these carpets. So the result is that there in one room was a stack of carpets from the ground up. Now what's the use of buying a whole pile of carpets? You put it all in a great pile, which in fact takes up all the space in the room, and you can't even see the other carpets except the one on top. So what was the point of buying thousands and thousands of dollars worth of carpets just to stack them up there? And year after year, the pile was there and getting bigger, because they didn't seem to know what else to buy and to use their money for.

They were not Christians. They had no real aim in life. And so what are you going to do with your money? They didn't care much for traveling. What was there to do? I mean, apart from going out and having a big meal, which was very bad for their health, because they were already a bit oversized. Another big meal was not going to do their health any good. It would only serve to shorten their life, because that was the only other enjoyment they knew, was to eat, rather like Hong Kong people understand very well. They eat. And then the size gets bigger, the heart condition gets worse. Eventually the wife developed heart trouble, diabetes, and all the list goes on and on. And the husband had all kinds of physical problems as well. So they had everything and really nothing.

And I observed this and thought -- and I was of course not a Christian myself, I was in my early teens then -- I thought to myself, how meaningless, how very meaningless is this life? What is the point of having this enormous wealth and not having the faintest clue what to do? I mean, there wasn't any point buying another house and another house. There was no point buying many cars. I mean, how many cars can you drive? You can only drive one car at a time anyway. And they were not interested in cars. Neither of them even knew how to drive. And since they lived in the city, where they could walk to anywhere they wanted to go, everything is very convenient there, no use for a car at all. I looked at the situation, I thought to myself, what is the meaning of life anyway for them, and for anyone floating around in this wealth?

And so, when I came to the Lord, I read there in 1 Corinthians 13, a passage that I'm sure you are very familiar with. That is the chapter about love. And a passage that it is very important for us to think deeply on, to meditate on. And perhaps we can read a few verses from this important chapter, even if we think we are quite familiar with it. Because it is precisely the familiar passages that we often understand the least.

I hope you will excuse me for speaking relatively slowly, because I know that the translators have a very hard time. And I have the tendency to speak very fast when I preach, so I have to deliberately slow myself down. I used to have a translator in England that when I preached, my translator translated for me every Sunday. He is often out of breath, just trying to keep up with me. Since then, I tried to learn to slow down.

1 Corinthians 13: "If I speak in the tongue of men and of angels" -- can you speak in the tongue of angels? I think not. But it says, "but have not love, I am a noisy gong or a clanging cymbal. And if I have prophetic powers and understand all mysteries and knowledge" -- you may be a scholar. You have a lot of knowledge. You understand a lot of mysteries. If you are in physics, you might understand a lot of mysteries of this universe. They really are mind-boggling. I often read books on physics out of pure interest in creation. And the ideas are often mind-boggling. Amazing. Things like quantum physics and so on. It has astonishing ideas. But if you have all this knowledge, and if I have even faith -- you may be a wonderful Christian. You have enormous faith. Faith that is far above the ordinary Christian. You have faith so as to be able to remove mountains. Oh, that is something. Who has got this kind of faith here? Which one here has faith that can remove mountains? Not to say great Himalaya mountains. Let's talk about little mountains like Mount Royal. Can you move mountains? "But if I have this kind of faith, but have not love, I am nothing."

"And if I give all I have away, and if I deliver my body to be burned, but have not love, I gain nothing." I am nothing. But I don't have love. And you say, wait a minute, wait a minute. Now, President Bush, George Bush is President of the United States, right? Of course he is. And he has overwhelming love, right? Who said? Nobody said he is love. He probably doesn't have any more love than any of us here. And then how can we say if he doesn't have love, he is nothing. He is President of the United States, and that's really something. Or if you were a scholar or professor, and you have some position in the world, and you don't have love -- does your professor have love? No, he has no love. He gives you a hard time. He grinds your nose in the dust. He makes you work overtime. Your eyes are blinking all red with tiredness. And he says, "This project needs to be done by tomorrow." This fellow is not human. I mean, he doesn't remember his time as a student. Or maybe he does remember. That's because his professor was grinding his face in the papers. So now he is going to take it out on me. Well, if you want to revenge, don't revenge on me. Revenge on your professor. But he can't reach his professor now. So you are the poor scapegoat. This guy is not human. But is he nothing? Of course he is something. He is your boss. What do you mean, I am nothing? He is nothing? Of course he is something. Maybe I am nothing, but he is something. And he has no love? No, no.

What we've got to understand here, brothers and sisters, is this: that without love, no one, including George Bush, is anything in God's sight. This is the point. It is looking from God's point of view. Of course in the eyes of the world, the president is still the president. The professor is still the professor. He still has his position, his honor. He is really something in the world. But in God's sight, without love, they are nothing. And we are nothing.

Now this point is very important to understand. Because it means that God's way of evaluation is entirely different from man's way. Try to grasp this point very clearly. If you are something, even in the church, you have faith, you have prophetic powers, you are something. Because if you have prophetic powers, if you can speak with a tongue of angels, if you have knowledge to understand spiritual mysteries, let me tell you, you are really something in the church. Are you not? You are. Of course you are. You can open the Bible and expound it and people's mouth drop open. This man is amazing. He's incredible. He opens the Bible and he can just show you the meanings. "I never saw this in my life." He has the understanding of mysteries, just as Paul himself had. Paul had the knowledge of mysteries. He had the faith that could move mountains. And it is he who tells us that in God's sight all this is nothing. Without love. Nothing.

In God's sight, you may be a Christian worker or pastor or something. I tell you, in God's sight, you are nothing. Unless you have love. Unless you have His love. Now that is something amazing. This changes our whole concept. In other words, when I look at you or I look at a Christian worker, I listen to somebody's preaching, I'm not going to say, "Oh, this is a wonderful man of God because his preaching is so eloquent." If you think like this, you are thinking with human wisdom. "He's so eloquent. He's a great man of God. He can perform miracles. He has insight into the mystery of things." The Lord gives me insight too. I don't think it's because I'm that good. It's because He has mercy upon others.

Not so long ago, there was someone who applied for the six-team training. Someone I didn't even really know because this time there are quite a number of people applying for training that I don't really know. In fact, some of them I've never even talked to because the churches are so many these days and the numbers of people are so large. Since our churches now, many of them have hundreds or more people in Hong Kong or elsewhere, we are now speaking of hundreds of people, and among them there are many people I don't really know. And this person was one of them. I didn't really know. And applied for the full time. What can I do? In the past, I used to interview candidates. Now to interview them, I have to travel on three continents. I could travel around the world to interview them. So I leave the co-workers to do the assessment and then I will study the assessment, put it before the Lord and if I have questions I will ask the co-workers to clarify.

And as I prayed and looked to the Lord for instruction about this person that I didn't know who had applied for full time, the word of the Lord came to me and said, "There is something in that person's life that needs to be put right, and until that is put right, that person cannot be accepted."

# Love (Part 2)

The co-workers overseas and said the Lord has given me the word that this person has something to put right, and until that is done the person cannot be accepted. So the co-workers passed the message on to this person and a few days later I received a phone call from the other side of the world from this person, and the first thing that was said to me on the phone was, "I am shocked." He said, "I am shocked at this." And I said, "Well, have you put the matter right?" And the person said, "Yes, after I received that word from the Lord through you, I have speedily put the matter right." I didn't want to know what the matter was. I am not curious to know people's personal matters. Sometimes the Lord reveals to me what the matter is when someone hides a sin and it is revealed to me in order to save that person. In this case I didn't want to know. But the person still wanted to tell me. "It's all right, it's up to you if you want to tell me." But the main thing is put it right. And it having been put right, then I said to that person, "Since you have now put the matter right, you can be accepted into the training."

Though I have knowledge, it says here, of all mysteries. Because how do you know a mystery? The only way you can know a mystery is for God to reveal it to you. A mystery by definition is something you know only by revelation. There is no human way to know this. There is no way I knew what that person's problem was by human means. But God reveals these things to us. Does that mean I am a great servant of God? No. Believe me, it does not mean anything of the kind. It doesn't.

Because here I must impress on you, beloved brothers, that the only thing that means anything in the sight of God is whether there is love in us. It doesn't matter whether you have these amazing gifts from God. It doesn't matter. On that day, whether you are anything or not depends only on this: that God's love controls your being. And though you may not be able to speak with the tongues of men and of angels, though you may not know any mysteries, though you don't have faith that can move mountains, your faith is only a grain of mustard seed, even less than that. Because the Lord said if you had that much faith, you could do amazing things. Though your faith is very small, but your heart is open to His love. Then and only then you are something. You are something.

In other words, God evaluates man by this one standard alone. When you read this passage, you must understand this. We are something or we are nothing. Depending on whether God's love has control of our life. You think that's easy? No one who has seriously -- I mean seriously, not in some superficial or shallow sense -- really tried to let God's love control his heart or her heart, no one who has seriously tried that will know, will say or think that love is easy. It is in fact not only not easy, it is impossible, as we shall see in a moment. It's not possible. In other words, the only way that you can have this kind of love, as distinct from human love, is for God to pour that love into your heart. In other words, it has to be a miracle of transformation. There is no way we can produce this love ourselves. To be anything in God's sight, it has to start with God's transforming work, to change you around from a person who is self-centered, selfish, and therefore hating and being hated, to somebody who is able to love in a genuine way. That is exceedingly difficult.

Now, very quickly, I want to take you to the realization that there are two kinds of love. And we are always in danger of confusing these two. There is human love, and there is God's love. And these two are entirely different. They are radically and fundamentally different. And yet, often, in outward appearance, they look alike. They look alike outwardly. And they can fool other people. But they cannot fool God. And that is why even if you outwardly appear to be very loving, very kind, very considerate, look at what it says here: "If I give away all that I have," it says in verse 3, "I even give my body to be burned, but have not love." Not God's love. I am motivated by some other kind of love, namely a human love. In God's sight, I still have nothing. I have nothing. I gain nothing. And therefore, I am nothing.

How many people, out of devotion to a national cause, have given their body to be burned? Indeed they have. Quite literally. I am sure you still remember the things that happened in Vietnam, when certain monks poured gasoline all over themselves and literally gave their body to be burnt, to make some political protest. In that way also they become martyrs in the history of their country. They get actually a little paragraph or even one line in the history books. Because it's so unusual for somebody to set themselves alight. They literally give their body to be burnt. And of course if you give your body to be burnt, you also have given away everything you have. You've got nothing left once your body is gone. But is that love for God? Is that God's love? No. That is not necessarily God's love. It may be, it may not be. We are not in a position to judge. All I am saying is that it may not be. It is only God who looks on the heart.

We can do very radical things out of sheer anger or frustration or hatred. Quite amazing. Anyone who goes out to kill somebody knows that if they are caught they will die too. They will also be destroyed. But they will still do it. Literally they give their own life in order to take revenge. Giving oneself may not be out of love at all. If you get caught and put away in jail all your possessions don't do you any good. You have given away everything but just in order to gain revenge.

So we can go on with examples of this. It can also, and we must bear this in mind, that somebody goes into full-time service, gives away everything they have. But believe me, it is not always motivated by the right reason. It may be that one feels that one has really no meaning in life, and one wants to gain some sort of meaning. And serving in the church is very meaningful. Being a church leader does bring a lot of meaning and to some extent satisfaction, although also precious. So one can give up, so to say, one's profession and therefore with it one's possessions. But might it sometimes be motivated by a certain kind of ambition that is not necessarily spiritual? The answer is sometimes yes, in my experience, yes, unfortunately. It is not always motivated by love. And those of you who go into full-time service, I ask you to think carefully on this message today. Without love, in God's sight you are nothing. You may have the best training in the world. And that can bring you pride. Because you are so skilled in the word of God. You are indeed a workman, skilled to the extent you don't need to be ashamed. You can be proud of the excellence of your work. You can exegete a scripture and people look at it and admire the marvel of it, the insight, the depth. But again, I beg of you to remember that in God's sight that makes you nothing. He looks for only one thing and one thing alone: whether His love is in your heart.

Human love is self-centered. And therefore you can test yourself. If the reason for doing what you do is basically selfish, or to some extent selfish -- because sometimes our motives are very mixed, so that we have difficulty even discerning our own motives -- but if it is to any degree selfish, then it is not of God. Because human love is selfish. That's why I said there are two distinct kinds of love. And they often look alike but they are not the same. Outwardly they appear alike. A person can be very helpful, very kind. And there are non-Christians who are very kind and very helpful. And I dare not judge what motivates their love. Giving can give a lot of satisfaction. It is psychologically very good to do that. In psychotherapy, one of the things you ask people to do is go out and help others. It does wonders for your health, both your mental health and your physical health. And therefore there are those who do these things, helping other people, doing social work, mainly for their own sake. It gives them tremendous satisfaction.

I once talked to a doctor who was a psychiatrist. As you know, a psychiatrist is a qualified doctor who specializes in medicine relating to the mind, to the brain. And in the process, he was a very clever man. He could solve, or try to solve, his own psychological problems by studying psychiatry. So you see, people do not necessarily study even medicine, or psychiatric medicine, because they love people or care for people so much. Because it's out of self-concern, first and foremost. Of course, because we are close friends, he would tell me that. I don't think he would tell his patients anything like this.

Love, helping others, showing concern, spending time with them, is not necessarily motivated by God's love. It may be, it may not be. Or there may be mixed motives in there. The other thing about human love is that it is short-lived. It is self-centered, and it is short-lived. It doesn't last for long. That is the tendency for it to die very quickly.
