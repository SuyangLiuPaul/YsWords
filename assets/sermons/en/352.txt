# Faith: Seeing the Unseen (Part 1) — The Spiritual Sight That Brings Divine Approval

We have been for some time now in this church expounding on the scriptural teaching of regeneration and renewal. And in this way to lay a very firm foundation for everyone here as to what being a Christian is all about. And today I would like to continue in this series on regeneration and renewal, but to expound on the meaning of faith, the character of faith in the New Testament. Because faith is central to any discussion about regeneration and renewal.

Now, expounding faith is no simple matter. And many books have been written on the subject. But what I shall aim to do is to expound faith from an angle which we have not done so far. Maybe going further back to the foundations of faith. We have already defined faith in the past as commitment, as total commitment, as faithfulness of character, faithfulness of our commitment. To keep faith, for example, means quite simply — to keep faith means to be faithful. So we know that faith has its essential meaning as faithfulness, and more specifically as commitment. A total commitment to God and to the brother or to the neighbor. But I'd like to, if possible, go even further back than this. To something even more fundamental than this, if possible. And I would try to do this in a number of hopefully clearly defined points. And to establish each one of these points as we go along. Not so that you have a better intellectual grasp of the meaning of faith. But so that you will know how to enter into faith. Because my concern is very practical. And not at all theoretical or intellectual. I'm concerned that we are able to live the life of faith. What do we want to live the life of faith for? In order that we may know the living God. Because without faith, there is no way to know God. So we are dealing with something very basic. But something more important than which we cannot find in this world.

We die to sin, right? We die to sin because unless we die to sin we cannot go on into the spiritual life." But the matter is not quite as simple as this. You see, you cannot die to sin until you die to the flesh. Now this point is very important for you to grasp, and I say this to those being baptized as to all of you: there is no way you are going to die to sin until you die to the flesh. Now I would have thought that Christians would find this obvious. Not so obvious, as I have discovered.

Let us turn to Romans 7, in the remaining minutes that we have before we close. In Romans chapter 6, Paul has been speaking about baptism and death to sin. In Romans chapter 7, he points out that nobody can die to sin without having died to the flesh. Now let us look at this passage very carefully. We start again from the well-known verse in verse 17. Perhaps we will go back to verse 15 again. It's so important to get the context. "I do not understand my own actions." In verse 15 of Romans 7, Paul says, "For I do not do what I want, but I do the very thing I hate." I want to do good. I make a New Year's resolution, and I discovered that my resolution was good, but I couldn't keep it. I'm sure you have discovered that already. You want to do a good thing, and you find that you can't do it. You're overpowered by something in yourself. You want to do this good thing, and you end up doing something not so good, indeed bad. Verse 16: "Now if I do what I do not want, I agree that the law of God is good. So then it is no longer I that do it, but sin that dwells in me." Now sin is living in me. Why don't I do the good that I want to do? Because there is some power inside of me. Sin is not just an action; it is a power. And that power is compelling me to do something that I didn't want to do.

Verse 18: "For I know that nothing good dwells within me, that is, in my flesh." Please notice these words: "nothing good dwells in me, that is, in my flesh." "I can will what is right, but I cannot do it. For I do not do the good I want, but the evil that I do not want, that I do. Now if I do what I do not want, it is no longer I that do it, but sin that dwells in me." And so he goes on explaining this point, and then to verse 23: "But I see in my members" — the members of my physical body; we speak of our arm, our legs; these are members, and this, when used as a plural, refers to the body generally — "but I see in my members another law at work, warring with the law of my mind, and making me captive to the law of sin which dwells in my members." I can see that which dwells in my body. And so the last line of chapter 7: "With my flesh I serve the law of sin." Those are the tragic closing words of chapter 7. "With my flesh, my physical body, I serve the law of sin."

Now you can see that what I said earlier — there is no way you will get rid of sin without getting rid of the flesh — it's quite plain. Now you understand why in baptism there is death. Death in baptism represents the putting off of the flesh, to be finished with life in the flesh. And that is why we come to Romans chapter 8, verse 9. And this is what it says. After you have put off the flesh in baptism and have received the Holy Spirit, what does Paul say? Romans 8, verse 9: "But you are not in the flesh, you are in the Spirit, if in fact the Spirit of God dwells in you." You are not in the flesh anymore. That's a remarkable statement. You say, "I'm not in the flesh?" It looks as though I'm still in the flesh. But again, Paul is speaking spiritually. You have put off the control, the power of the flesh. He doesn't mean you have committed suicide through baptism. What He means is that you have finished with a life that has been dominated by the flesh, controlled by the flesh. Now you are in the Spirit. That is, controlled by the Spirit.

Christians are a defeated bunch today. They live in defeat, and they are wondering, "It seems to me Romans 7 is true of me. I try to do good, and I'm doing evil." That's right, because you have not been freed from the dominion of the flesh. Now the flesh — so long as you are in the flesh, you are under the power of sin, as Romans chapter 6 has been saying through the whole chapter. And because you are in the flesh, the flesh is like a veil, a veil covering your face, so that you cannot see the things which are spiritual. You cannot. No man in the flesh can see spiritual things. He cannot. And you will know right now whether spiritual things are real to you, or whether they are just mythological to you. Because to the man in the flesh, spiritual things are just mythological. They are just ideas, fancies, philosophies. They are not real. What's real? Real is this material. This is real. This body is real. This pillar is real. This wall is real. These are real. The things that I can see, I can feel, I can touch, I can smell, I can taste — these are real. But spiritual things? Oh no.

And that is why Paul says in 1 Corinthians that spiritual things and the things of God are foolishness to the natural man. That is, they don't make sense to the natural man. The only reality is physical reality. That is why becoming a Christian is a fundamental change. It is a fundamental change. And I would like you to bear this in mind when, in a few moments, the baptism takes place. Because what they are doing is putting off the flesh through baptism. Now baptism has no magic. There's no magic. We don't have any special acids in there that will dissolve the flesh when they go in there. I am sure you can have any chemist to test the water, so there is no dangerous acids in there. No, no. The transaction has taken place on the spiritual level. That is, through faith, at baptism they are putting off from their lives the control of the flesh and putting themselves now under the control of the Spirit. That is a transfer.

Now bear carefully in mind: so long as the flesh covers your sight, your spiritual sight, spiritual things will never be real to you. Turn to 2nd Corinthians 3, and I must close on this note. 2nd Corinthians chapter 3, and verse 15. "Yes, to this day" — 2nd Corinthians 3:15 — "to this day, when Moses is read, a veil" — notice the word veil — "lies over their mind. But when a man turns to the Lord, the veil is removed." Do you know what that veil is? Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom. "And we all, with unveiled face, beholding the glory of the Lord" — can you behold the glory of the Lord? Can you see the glory of the Lord? No way, not until the veil is removed. But it is through the Lord that the veil is removed. When a man turns to the Lord, verse 16 — how is the veil removed? When he turns to the Lord, the veil of the flesh is removed, and he beholds with unveiled face the glory of the Lord. And what happens? "And we are being changed. We are being changed from glory to glory, for this is of the Lord who is the Spirit."

What is the veil? All through this chapter, Paul is speaking about a veil. And then in chapter 4, he is still talking about the veil. Chapter 4, verse 3: "And even if our gospel is veiled" — if the message I am preaching to you is veiled today, it is veiled only to those who are perishing. They are perishing. This gospel, this message makes no sense to them at all. The veil is on their face. And if the message, Paul says, we are preaching is veiled, it is veiled to those who are perishing. Why? Because they are in the flesh. If you are under the bondage of sin, Paul tells us in Romans, the wages of sin is death; therefore you are perishing. Now it is in Christ that this veil is removed, the veil of the flesh. And Hebrews chapter 10, verse 20, speaks of "the veil of his flesh." The veil is compared to the veil of the flesh in our body.

Now I hope now you have got the picture together, and hope that you will understand the meaning of baptism much more clearly. And now you will see why it is essential for every Christian to put away the old way of life dominated by the flesh, preoccupied with the things which are seen — with cars and houses and TVs and radios and this and that and everything physical — and spiritual things, they are all unreal to them. It becomes intellectual exercises. It becomes believing at second hand, because somebody else believes, so I believe. You know what's wrong with that? What's wrong is that it costs you nothing to believe at second hand. A person who sees with unveiled face the glory of the Lord has had to do away with the flesh. And that is costly. Baptism, in its spiritual meaning, is very costly. But you can believe at second hand, and it costs you nothing, because you believe on the basis of somebody else's belief. You don't have to do away with the flesh for intellectual faith. Do you see that? For intellectual believing, you don't have to remove the flesh, because you will not directly see. You believe on the basis of the witness of somebody else. But there is where the Lord Jesus speaks to us in Matthew 16, verse 24: "Except a man deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me." If you take up your cross, you are finished with the flesh. He cannot be my disciple. You see? Nobody can deny himself who is merely committed to intellectual faith. Intellectual faith costs nothing. In fact, it's very interesting. It's very exciting. You can toy around with philosophical ideas or religious ideas. It costs you nothing to play around with ideas. In fact, when you're bored, it's very interesting to fool around with ideas. But spiritual faith means the removal of the veil of the flesh through death, the death of the old way of life in the flesh. Can you see? Are spiritual things real to you?

So let me close then with Hebrews chapter 12, verse 2: "Looking to Jesus." You cannot even fulfill that command. "Looking to Jesus" — it's a command impossible of fulfillment, until the veil of the flesh is removed. How can you look to Jesus? How can Jesus be real to you? No way it can be done.

In closing, let me say one more time: faith in the New Testament is the opposite of physical sight, but like physical sight, it is its own proof. If you ask me, "How do you know that this place or this person exists?" my answer is very simple: I have seen him. Sight is its own evidence. I see; therefore I know. How do I know that God is the living God, that He is real? Because by the grace of God, with the eyes of faith, I see Him. He is so real to me that, as far as this physical world is concerned, it can pass; it doesn't matter. For Paul, Jesus was so real that he was happy to go to be with Jesus, as he says in Philippians 1 — happy to go to be with Him and leave this whole world behind. He said, "I have suffered the loss of everything," in Philippians 3, "and count it as rubbish," because what he has seen of the glory of the Lord is far more meaningful to him than the houses and cars and all this stuff. He says, "I count all that as garbage." He is looking forward to gain, to win Christ.

Do you have this faith that is saving? It is by this faith that we are justified. By this faith, as Hebrews 11:2 tells us, that we will be acceptable to God, approved by God.
