And even if you are wearing a heavy coat, you can really feel the cold at something really that is life threatening.

It happens from time to time in Canada that somebody's car breaks down on some country road, and they freeze to death. That kind of Christianity is all there is that most Christians have. They reject salvation by a law of works, but they accept salvation by law as a gift. Free justification is an act of the law. And they do not accept that there is anything more to it than that, that we are saved on the basis of a de jure act of God. Strange. As somebody who studies theology and has studied it for a long time, I find this quite mystifying in many ways. The same people who tell us that we are not saved by the law finally tell us that we are after all saved by a legal act of God which is law.

So in closing I want to give you ten points very quickly and very briefly as a guideline. And I want to tell you again once more why we must reject any teaching that limits salvation to one form of law or another. I have given the illustration before. For example, a person is a drug addict and he is caught by the police in possession of drugs. In Malaysia, if you carry more than a few grams of drugs on you, you are sentenced to death. It is a mandatory death sentence for which there is even no appeal. And that applies even to foreigners who are not Malaysian citizens but caught in Malaysia carrying drugs, they are sentenced to death. Recently as you know an Australian woman was sentenced to death in Malaysia for carrying drugs, very small quantity of drugs but above what is permitted, above the amount which could have spared her from a death sentence.

And I have given this illustration before. If you are a drug addict and you are caught in possession of drugs and you are sentenced to death, and somebody says, I will take the death sentence instead of you — even if the judge accepts that and the other person is sentenced to death, you are free. You are acquitted because of somebody else dying in your place. And so we are told the same thing. We have committed sin worthy of death before God and Christ has died in our place and we thank him for it. And this is a valuable part of the gospel. I would not minimize it in any way. But I will say to you that if that is the entire gospel, then so help us God, because we are finished. Because the drug addict, even if he is acquitted from the death penalty, is still a drug addict. And what will he do? He will go out again and be taking drugs again and he will be sentenced to death again and who is going to die for him the next time? Or will somebody keep dying for him? Or will the death of Christ avail for any number of sins after conversion? And I am talking about mortal sins of this kind. No, that is the one alternative. So we are always saved by law. So we hope. So we think. But is that what the Bible teaches?

We need a salvation that not just acquits us from sin. We need a salvation that gives us grace to overcome sin. To set us free from our drug addiction to sin. Is it not? What kind of a God is it who would play such a cruel joke upon us, who will set us free from the guilt of sin but not set us free from the power of sin? And what will be his excuse if he has the power to set us free from sin, from the power of sin, to set us free from our drug addiction, and yet will not do it? What kind of a game is this that he wants to play on us? Anyone who thinks like this must not have been reading Romans chapter 8 and much of the rest of the Bible. The glory of the gospel is not just that we have been acquitted legally but that we have been freed from the power of sin.

Let me give you another illustration quickly. There was a recent case which I have here before me from the newspaper and I pondered this case. This case, some of you may have read about, concerning a rather good-looking model, a fashion model, and some man wanted to date her but she turned him down. And he was so upset about this, at being turned down by this good-looking model, you know what he did? He hired two thugs not just to beat her up but to literally carve up her face. And so these two thugs, one held down this girl and the other one carved her face up with a knife, and you know that they had to put in more than 150 stitches in her face to stitch up the wounds. She was so utterly disfigured, this beautiful girl.

And these two thugs were apprehended and three of them, the one who hired the other two and the three of them, were taken before a court in Manhattan, New York. That's why somebody said, if you want to go to school in Manhattan, New York, go to school in New York. I said New York is just not the place to go. I mean, the crime rate is unbelievable. But anyway, all this happened in New York, and the court sentencing these three criminals — what else can you call them — who in their despicable conduct of selfishness and greed and with no regard for anybody else, because he could not have this girl, he decided that nobody is going to have her and so he just carved her up. That's how much love amounts to selfishness as it is.

Well, the court sentenced these criminals and awarded this model damages amounting to 78 million US dollars. 78 million US dollars in damages was awarded to her for suffering, for loss of past income, loss of future income, loss of her good looks, as well as the medical fees that she had to pay and has to pay. 78 million US dollars works out at over 600 million Hong Kong dollars. 600 million Hong Kong dollars in damages. But you know what's the tragedy about this story? This poor girl in fact does not get a cent. Not one cent. Legally, yes. Legally she owns 608 million Hong Kong dollars. Legally, de jure as it's called, or some Americans say de jure — she owns, she's worth hundreds of millions of dollars. De facto, in fact, she doesn't get a cent. Why? Well, because the three thugs don't have any money. As simple as that. They don't have any money, nor will they ever have any money, why? Because this law will be in effect only for 20 years and these thugs will be in jail for up to 15 years. Now that's the marvelous working of the law, isn't it? You wonder whether this is a joke or whether this is serious. You award somebody 600 million Hong Kong dollars, but the fact is the person doesn't get a cent, and these criminals can't earn a cent because they'll be sitting in jail for the next 15 years. The latest sentence of five years, the others up to 15 years.

Now that's what I'm trying to bring home to you. What use is it to you to be awarded the whole world in damages, if you like, when in the end you will get absolutely nothing because you yourself fall back into sin and live under bondage to sin for the rest of your life? What kind of a gospel is this? I don't want to preach a gospel like this. It turns the gospel into a joke, into a farce, just like this legal enactment — looks beautiful on paper, no practical value whatsoever. None whatsoever. Can you see my point?

Becoming a Christian has to do with a living relationship with God in which he not just legally sets you free from sin and punishes the culprit, let's say Satan in this case — what's the use if Satan goes to hell for a million years or forever? It's no good to me that he goes to hell. It's no use to this girl who has her face carved up, who's gone through plastic surgery, but the plastic surgeon says that some of the scars will be there for life, cannot be permanently removed. What use is it to her to have the culprit sentenced when the real problem is not dealt with? The real problem is sin and it can't be dealt with in this kind of way, not by some act of law. We must grasp this point.

I preach a gospel, and the New Testament preaches a gospel, that tells you not just that you are forgiven of your sins but you are going to live in bondage to sin for the rest of your life and that's too bad, but that God has given us the power through the Holy Spirit to live victoriously and let the world see it. Have you seen Christians who live miserable lives, defeated lives? Of course you have. And that's why you don't want to be a Christian. That's why I didn't want to be a Christian for so many years. It's when I saw Christians who were free from the bondage of sin — not that they never committed anything wrong, but they were free from the power of sin, they were able to overcome it — when I saw that kind of Christians, that turned my life around. Because I saw the power of God in action, not just in some legal statement that doesn't affect anybody, but in action that just doesn't only say, I am acquitted. It enables me to live a life that is full of meaning, full of joy, victorious. A God that I am able to know, to fellowship with, and who makes himself real to me through my walking with him.

I must close, but let me quickly give you a ten-point guideline which will only take me two or three minutes, because this guideline will be very important for you as a means of detecting false teaching, detecting whether a teaching is of Antichrist or not. First point: God, not self, is the center of the true Christian's life. Second point: Jesus is Lord, not just Savior, of our lives. Thirdly: Christ delivered us not just from the guilt of sin but from the power of sin. Fourthly: grace is God's enabling gift to us in the form of power to live victoriously. It is not just a free gift of forgiveness or a free gift that we can use as we please. Grace is God's enabling power, and God is real to us because we experience his power in our lives. How else can God be real to you? He cannot become real to you through an enactment of the law that you have never experienced for yourself. Fifthly: justification is not just a declaring to be righteous de jure but a making righteous and enabling to be righteous de facto. Sixthly: faith is a total commitment of our hearts to God and not just an intellectual assent to certain Christian doctrines. Seventhly: faith or commitment is a continuous thing and not just a once-and-for-all thing. Eighthly: we are called to a new quality of life in the present time and not just a salvation in the future. Ninthly: the New Testament affirms, indeed the whole Bible affirms, that holiness is obligatory, not optional. Tenthly, and finally: salvation in the Bible is communal, that is, in the body of Christ. It is not just personal. It is not just personal salvation that we can talk about. Salvation does not exist personally if it means that it is outside the body of Christ. Salvation is always communal. It always takes place within the living body of Christ, the church. And that means that our commitment to God must always, without exception, be reflected in our commitment to the brothers and sisters in the church. There is no commitment to God that does not involve my commitment also to you, to my brothers and sisters here in this church and in any church where Christ is Lord. These are the ten points which, if we fully understand, we shall not be deceived by the Antichrist.
