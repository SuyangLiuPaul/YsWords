# The Cross and the Antichrist (Part 1)

To talk about the Antichrist is not so simple because I have to give you some background information. On the other hand, I don't want to speak about the Antichrist in some academic fashion. I want to show that it applies to your life. I want to speak to start by talking about the Resurrection. What it has to do with the Antichrist will become evident to you in a little while.

There is such a thing as the Resurrection. And I'm not just talking about the future Resurrection, which will also take place, and I mentioned it a moment ago. I'm talking about the fact that when Jesus came into the world, something happened. A unique phenomenon appeared. A phenomenon of the Resurrection. You know, in the Old Testament, the word Resurrection — the Old Testament section of the Bible, the Jewish Bible — the word Resurrection doesn't even appear, not once. There are some hints and references to the Resurrection, but the word doesn't appear at all.

When you turn to the New Testament, a complete change. The word Resurrection, Anastasis in the New Testament, the Greek word, occurs 42 times. 42 times in the New Testament. Not to mention many other words referring to the Resurrection, like "He is risen," or "one is raised up," and other such related words. It runs into hundreds of times. The Resurrection from the dead — it's a key idea. A key fact, in fact, in the New Testament.

Some years ago, when I was in England in student days, I met somebody called Dr. Lowe. In fact, I think Brother Jeffrey, who is on duty today and cannot be here — Brother Jeffrey also knows Dr. Lowe. Dr. Lowe was a very fine brother, a doctor from China, who went to England to do his medical examinations, because his medical training in China was not recognized in this part of the world. And so he had to take his exams all over again. And he shared with me on one occasion how, as a young doctor, he was assigned to a certain part of Guangdong, a certain village in Guangdong province. And there he had to look after a number of patients. He was known to be a Christian, because he everywhere proclaimed the glory of his Lord.

And he was looking after an elderly lady. And the worst thing that can happen to a young doctor in his first practical assignment — the patient died on him. The patient died. And he thought to himself, this is a little village, and everybody knows I'm a Christian, and my patient dies on me. He was desperate. He didn't know what to do. He tried everything he knew from his medical training to revive his patient again. All in vain. All in vain.

He was so anxious and so desperate. He ran to one — there was one local doctor there — and he rushed there. He told me that in his anxiety, he stumbled and fell over a few times, picked himself up from the ground, and came panting to the doctor, and said, "Please, please help me." And the doctor said, "Well, what happened?" He said, "My patient died." And the doctor said, "Well, what are you asking me for?" He said, "But she's dead. You've got to help me." He said, "Don't you understand? She's dead. What are you asking me? Do you think I'm a magician? Do you think I'm God? I can raise the dead? If she's dead, she's dead." He said, "But please, you've got to do something." He said, "Well, what do you expect me to do?"

So very dejected, the young Dr. Lowe went back, crying out to the Lord, went back to his patient, checked his patient, and she was still dead. He said, "Lord, what am I going to do? Please help me. What's going to happen to my witness in this village? What's going to happen to me?" He fell on his knees, the tears running down his face, crying out to the Lord, and he prayed and he prayed. And suddenly he heard a cough and a groan, and he looked at the woman, and there was some movement in her eyes, in her eyelids, and there was some color coming back into her face. And he said, you know what happened? She came back to life.

Now this is a doctor. If you and I thought somebody was dead, maybe we might be wrong. We don't know how to tell the difference between somebody being dead and alive, perhaps. We've got trouble even telling that spiritually. We have a hard time telling whether a Christian is dead or alive spiritually. And some Christians don't know whether they are dead or alive spiritually. But at least you expect that a doctor knows whether his patient is dead or alive. She came back to life.

But anyway, I go to these bookshops, and I saw a book on sale. Mostly I only look for relatively serious theological works. And this book was not a theological work, but it was going for $1.99. And I thought, for $1.99, this is rather irresistible. So I took this book back. And it proved to be very interesting. The whole book was describing the things that God has been doing in the lives of various people, the testimonies of various people of what God has done in their lives. But anyway, there was here in this book the testimony of Dr. William S. Reed, a surgeon in the United States, who tells a story about his two-year-old son who died of acute viral pneumonia, and died so suddenly and so quickly that — he went out, the son was suffering from fever — he went out to perform an operation, and when he came back, he found his son was already dead. He picked up his son, found, tested him for any sign of life, and he was dead. And this is what Dr. Reed tells us. He tried everything he knew according to his medical training to revive his son, but all in vain. He fell on his knees and he prayed and he wept before the Lord, crying out to the Lord. And as he wept and cried, as in the case of Dr. Lowe, there was a sign, a sound of movement, and the little boy, being dead for some ten minutes as far as the doctor was able to ascertain, came back to life. He put his stethoscope to the son's chest, and indeed, the son was back to life again. In fact, he tells his own story in a book which some of you may have seen. In fact, I have seen that book in Christian bookstores called The Surgery of the Soul.

Not long after his own son was resurrected, brought back to life by the power of the Lord, this surgeon was called to the pediatric section of the hospital in which he worked, and the nurse asked him to certify the death of a little girl. This is pretty routine, I guess, for doctors to be doing this all the time. And so he went into the bedside of this little girl who had died. He made the usual checks upon the girl for any signs of life. The girl had already died for a little while. And so he stopped the resuscitator and removed the IV from the girl's arm, the IV, the intravenous drip, removed the drip from her arm, and was about to sign the paper, the certificate of her death.

And then he saw the mother of that little girl, the mother of that little girl beside the bedside, heartbroken, sobbing and weeping. And Dr. Reed looked at the mother, and then it came back to his own mind how he himself had wept with the death of his two-year-old son a few weeks earlier. And compassion — the compassion of the Lord came into his heart. And he felt, I can't just sign the death certificate. I can't just close the book like this. The Lord had mercy on me, and will I leave this mother weeping by the bedside of her little daughter?

And so he cried out to the Lord when he looked upon the dead girl lying there in the bed. And the words of the Lord came into his mind: "I am the Resurrection and the Life. He that believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live. And he that lives and believes in me shall never die." He put his hands on the little girl's head and he started to pray, calling upon the name of the Lord and saying to the Lord Jesus, "Lord Jesus, you gave me back my little boy. Will you not give back this little girl to her mother? Who else?" And the Lord did that. The little girl who had been dead for some time came back to life and was reunited with her mother.

These are witnesses, not fairy tales, not stories that we have drawn up from somewhere else. These are by responsible people who are living today. I have even mentioned his name a couple of times — Dr. William S. Reed. There are many such cases, and time hardly allows me to go on.
