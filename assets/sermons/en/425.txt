# The Meaning of Faith (2) - Part 1

Well, yesterday we spoke about the meaning of faith. We saw that faith is this complete commitment of ourselves to God. Today we must pursue this question somewhat further. We need to look at when we say we commit ourselves fully to Christ, and what is involved in saying so, and in making this kind of commitment. Today I seek by the grace of God to expound to you the Lord's teaching from Luke chapter 14, verse 25, on to verse 33.

Now these words, the Lord Jesus speaks to us. Things to be difficult words, and by God's grace, we have to face up to them. The Lord Jesus always speaks the truth to us. And even though the truth may be hard to take, He always speaks the truth.

Now we see in this passage that great multitude, verse 25 tells us, were following, were accompanying Jesus. The people knew that Jesus had something they needed. And everywhere that Jesus went, the great crowds were accompanying him. He was not a vote catcher. He was not looking to be popular. And so there were these great crowds following Jesus. And notice what He does. He turns around to the crowds and He speaks to them.

And notice what He says: "If anyone comes to me." He does not speak to some special people. He speaks to anyone. "If anyone comes to me." He is not speaking to His apostles or His disciples only. And so we must notice that these words are to every single person. And verse 27, He says, "Whoever does not bear his cross." So we can see that these words of Jesus are going to apply to you and to me. As He spoke this to the crowds, all those crowds, these were the people He was speaking to.

He said, "If anyone comes to me." Well, they were there with Him already. What does He mean, "come to me"? How do we come to Jesus? He clearly did not mean a physical coming because they were already there. But He meant a spiritual coming to Jesus. Some people are physically coming here today, but they have not spiritually come to Jesus.

And then when He speaks to these people who want to come, He says, "If you want to come to me spiritually, I'm going to tell you what is the condition." And then He speaks words which seem to be so difficult. What does He say? He says, "If anyone comes to me and does not hate his father and mother and wife and children and brothers and sisters, yes, even his own life, he cannot be my disciple."

Isn't that frightening all the people away? Yes, many people went away. Many people listened to Jesus once and they said, "The second time, we can't take it. What He says is too strong for me." And somebody said to me, "If you preach the gospel like this, as Jesus did, wouldn't it be that most of the people will not become Christians?" The Lord Jesus knows that. He didn't say, "You all come to me, it's okay. It doesn't matter how you believe, so long as you come to me, that's all right with me. Just come and fill the place up. I'm very happy when you're here." No, Jesus doesn't preach like this. But He says, "I'm going to tell you what is the condition." Those are hard words.

And so He says to them, except you hate all the people who are dear to you, including your own life, you cannot be His disciple. You cannot come to Him spiritually. Unless you do this, you cannot be a disciple of His, a follower of His.

Now, again, a disciple in the Gospels referred to any Christian. Any true Christian was called a disciple. It was not some special class of Christian. It was only later on, in Acts, that we find that the disciples were called Christians in Antioch. Who were called Christians? The disciples were called Christians. That means Christians and disciples, they are the same thing in the Bible. And that is why we saw that the opening words were addressed to everybody, the whole crowd.

But what did Jesus mean when He said, "You've got to hate your father and mother"? What does He mean by such frightening words? This is what Jesus said. We don't want to preach about this. Now, I know that many preachers, they don't want to preach about this. Let's look for another passage where it speaks about loving one another. Have you ever heard a preacher preaching on this passage? I've never heard a preacher preaching on this passage. So I'll be the first foolish man to preach on what Jesus taught us.

What did Jesus mean when He said that you cannot come to me spiritually unless you hate father, mother, and everybody, including your own life? Now in the Old Testament, you know that the fifth commandment says you shall honor your father and your mother. How can Jesus say that you need to hate them? I want to explain this to you because there are some enemies of the gospel who also like to take such passages and twist them, and young Christians become very confused.

Now in the Bible, we need to notice that Jesus had many enemies. And these enemies were always looking for some opportunity to attack Jesus. If He did some work on the Sabbath day, like His disciples picked a few grains of wheat, they'd say, "Ah, you're breaking the law." These enemies of Jesus, these Pharisees and scribes, they were always like vultures trying to find an opportunity to pick some bones. But if you notice, you'll find something interesting. Never once did the Pharisees and the Sadducees attack Jesus on what He said here. But if they wanted to attack Jesus, this would be a good thing to attack Him on. If they could say Jesus taught His disciples and everybody that they should not honor their father and their mother, and that they should break the fifth commandment, wow, they really got Jesus.

A strange thing is, Jesus' enemies never took the chance to attack Jesus for teaching this. You know why? Because the Jews understood what Jesus meant much better than anyone else. They knew exactly what He said. Jesus, of course, was not teaching any breaking of the fifth commandment, and the Jews knew that. In fact, in Mark chapter 7, verses 9 onwards, He rebuked the Pharisees and the scribes for not honoring their fathers and their mothers. So how can it be that He rebukes them for not honoring their father and their mother, and then says, if you want to come to me spiritually, you have to hate your father and mother?

So what did Jesus mean then? What did the Jews understand by what Jesus meant? Well, in the Jewish language, in the Jewish idiom, this hating somebody did not mean that you go out and you go and kill your father and mother. No, no, it didn't mean anything like this. It was an expression. When He says you must hate your own life also, it doesn't mean that you go to the revolving restaurant up there and then you jump down from there because you hate your own life. Jesus is not teaching us to commit suicide.

What this expression means is that when you are faced between the choice of two things, then what you choose is the one that you love more. And what you don't choose, that in the Hebrew expression was called to hate. So if you are forced to choose between two things, the one you choose is the one you love more, the one you don't choose is called hate. This is what we see also in Genesis chapter 29, for example. That Jacob loved one, and he hated the other of his wives. That doesn't mean that he really went and hated her and wanted to kill her. It means that he did not choose this one; when having to face between two, he preferred the one rather than the other.

Now once we understand this, we begin to see what Jesus is teaching us. This means that you can love both very much, but the one you prefer more is the one you love more, the one you don't prefer, that is the one in this Hebrew idiom, this way of speaking, that is called hate. Now what it means, what the Lord Jesus is saying to us is this: if you want to follow me, if you want to come to me spiritually, then you must put Jesus above everything else. You must love Jesus more than your father and your mother. You must love your father and mother. You must love them. But you must love God, you must love Jesus more than them. You must love Jesus more than even any member of your family who are close to you. And you must love Jesus even more than your own life.

So yesterday we saw that becoming a Christian is total commitment. And you may say, "Oh, total commitment, that's not so difficult. I just say, 'Lord, here I am. I commit myself totally to you.'" Not so quick. Jesus wants you to sit down and count the cost. Jesus doesn't want anyone just to rush in and say, "OK, I'm a Christian now." Two times in this passage Jesus says, sit down and count the cost. You notice that many times in big evangelistic meetings, people are swept by the emotion, the wonderful singing of the choir, and people feel so moved, they all rush to the front. And what Jesus says is, "No, no, no. Sit down. Sit down and count the cost." Total commitment is very costly.

What is the cost? The cost is to choose from now on, that whenever you are faced with a choice between God and your father, your mother, your wife, your children, yourself, you choose God. You say, total commitment is easy. Can you do that? You say you preach like this, all the Christians, which of us are going to be Christians? How hard is to become a Christian like this? But my friends, I didn't tell you this. Jesus tells us what He requires. We love our fathers and mothers very much. We love our wives and our children. We love our own life too. But if we are going to become a true Christian, Jesus says, you love them is right, but you will love God even more.

This means total commitment requires of us that God always comes first in your life. You say you are a Christian, then let me ask you, is God always first in your life? Can you honestly say that through this day, God has always been first in your life? Can you honestly say that God is more important than your studies, than your business, than your family?

Let me illustrate what the Lord Jesus means by an illustration that shows us that when called to the test, when we have to make a choice, the true Christian chooses God. This is a true account that is recorded for us, and which always moves me when I read that account. This account is found in the Jewish literature. This was the time when the Greek king in Syria was persecuting the Jews. And he tried to stop the Jews from worshiping God. And there was a family in which there was a mother and seven sons. They had a wonderful life together because they all loved God. They were united in God. Their hearts were devoted to God. No family was so united as they were. This father died, and the mother had brought up these seven sons. They were very precious.

And when the Greeks came to this place, they said all Jews must not worship their God anymore. If anyone was caught praying to their God, they would be put to death. Every family was tested. And this happened to this family. Now this family had been very happy. Now the Syrian and Greek armies brought this mother and these seven sons to the altar of idols. But not one person was willing to worship any idol. They were brought before the government official. He said, "Are you not going to worship our gods?" They said, "No."

So he said to the mother, "Well, if I can't persuade you to talk to the sons, maybe I will be able to persuade you by some action." So he took out the oldest son and put him to death in front of his mother and the other six. He said, "Now, do you think you are going to turn away from your God? Will you persuade your sons to turn away from God now? Do you enjoy seeing your sons dying? Do you want to see them all dying?" She said, "You can do what you like. I love my sons, but not one of them is going to turn from God."

And so one by one, down to the sixth, the fifth, the fourth, the third, everyone was put to death in front of the mother and the sons and all the people of the village. Not one son would turn from God. And it came to the youngest son. He was only a teenager at that time, a young teenager. The governor said, "Well, you are a young boy. You don't have to die. Your whole life is in front of you. You can just turn away from God and you will be alright." And the young boy said, "Don't waste your time. Just get on with it."

So he said to the mother, "Persuade your son." She spoke to him in Aramaic, which of course the Greeks couldn't understand, and told him, "Stand firm to the end, my son. I don't want to lose you." That means she didn't want to lose him in eternity. To lose him now meant that she would not lose him in eternity. She had to choose now. And she chose.

Which do you think would you call this: loving the son or hating the son? Of course she loves the son. But you can see now that these sons love their own lives. Of course they love their own lives. Nobody enjoys dying. But they loved God more than their own life. They hated, in comparison to their love for God, they hated their own life. In the end, the mother also was forced to death, having watched all her seven sons put to death in front of her. This is something that teaches us what it means, exactly what the Lord Jesus says, that when you have to be put to a choice, here you see that this mother and these sons, they loved God more than everything else, more than their own lives also.

This is the kind of commitment that Jesus requires of every person who is going to come to Him. This is devotion to God. If every person in the church had this kind of devotion to God, what kind of a church would we have today? What kind of Christians have we got in the church today? Oh, that God would speak to us. Jesus doesn't just want His churches to be filled up. He wants the men and the women of this kind of quality.

Think about it. That is the cost He requires of us when we come to Him. And when you thought that when we said yesterday that faith means total commitment, did you think that that was too high a standard that the Bible set for us? And let me ask you to look at Jesus' words and see for yourself what He said. I ask you to go home and look at these words again and see what they say to you. Jesus wants every Christian to be a dedicated man and woman.

How are we going to reach out in mission to the world with Christians who are neither hot nor cold? How can we be the light of the world when we are half darkness rather than light? And I hear people who want to say they want to bring the gospel to China. How are you going to bring the gospel to China? Do you know that the communists are some of the most dedicated people in the world? I say to you as I say to others: if the Christians today were to preach the gospel to the communists, they would laugh at you.

I was in China during what is known as the Korean War. And do you know, when the Chinese soldiers came back from the front, we had the most funny reports about the American soldiers and the United Nations soldiers. The American soldiers were always called the chocolate soldiers. That means to say, if they didn't get their daily ration of chocolate bar, they can't fight properly. And we had every kind of joke in China about these allied soldiers. The Chinese soldiers, you know, when they had nothing to eat, they took their leather belt, they boiled that and they ate their leather belts. They kept fighting. They went in there to fight, often hungry, often frozen, but they fought with dedication.

But the Americans required saturation bombing, they required long range artillery attack. No wonder, when I was in Hong Kong later on, there was an American Air Force officer. And some of our friends took him up to the Chinese border. He had just come from the Korean war. And we took him to the Chinese border and showed him, "That's China over there." He said, "Let's get going. Let's get out of here." So what's the matter? He said, "They frightened the wits out of me." Why? Because of the dedication of these soldiers.

And you want to be Christians? You want to preach the gospel to people with this kind of dedication? Would you be willing to chew your leather belt for Christ? Would you be willing to go through the icy waters of Korea, frozen, when even their trousers were frozen stiff into ice barrels, and they went on fighting the battle? How many Christians would be willing to do this?

I think that when the communists listen to Jesus' teaching, oh, that makes sense to them. Yes, of course you expect this kind of dedication. What else do you expect? But when you speak to the chocolate soldiers, you say, "Oh, this kind of thing, this is too high, this is too much." What kind of soldiers do we have in the church? We are going to go out and preach the gospel to these kind of people?

Any of you have heard of the 25,000 li long march, the 8,000 miles march that the communist soldiers made? I think the communists really put every Christian to shame. I feel ashamed of the church in talking to the communists. When we take them traveling, they cross several mountains and they have too much food. 8,000 li, they walked 8,000 li. We in the church go out hiking and we walk a few hills and those people already say, "Far enough." But these communist soldiers, they walked over 8,000 li.

They used to astonish me, some good sisters from Hong Kong. They seem always to be used to sitting on the bus. When I said, "Let's walk the three blocks down," "Wow, three blocks? No, no, no, we'll take a taxi." Oh, what Jesus requires of us is dedication. "You want to be my disciple? I tell you what I require of you."

And do you think Jesus wants His whole church full of these chocolate soldiers? No, He wants men of this kind of dedication. I tell you honestly, if I hadn't seen the Chinese Christians, the brothers and sisters in China, I would never have become a Christian. It is because I saw the lives of this kind of Christians, dedicated Christians, I said, "Lord, that's the kind of Christian I am ready to be. This is the only kind of Christian that really makes sense." That's why I often say, I thank God that the communists came to China. If the communists didn't come to China, I would never become a Christian. Because when the communists came to China, you know, the churches were full every Sunday. You squeeze in the church, so many people wearing nice jackets, nice hats, all come to church. And as the communist armies got nearer and nearer, the church got fuller and fuller, until the communists had arrived in Shanghai, and then the church was empty. Because the others got frightened, they started running very fast. And so the only kind of Christians that stood back, which remained, were the dedicated Christians.

I wonder how many of you would come into this meeting if you had found outside there was a desk at the front door, and there was a cadre sitting there to take your name and address down before you come in. I think very few would like to come in. I went to church one day and that's exactly what I found in Shanghai. One day I went to church and I saw at the door of the church the cadre was sitting there, taking the names of everybody coming in. And I saw some people coming to the church, they saw the cadre, quick turn back. There were only the few who went in.
