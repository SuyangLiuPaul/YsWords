# Abiding in Christ — External vs. Internal Connection; Antichrists Came from Within; What Internal Relationship Consists Of; God's Word as Living; Holy Spirit as Communicator

[Note: This is Part A of the sermon on John 15 and abiding, delivered 20 February 1983, Montreal. The series is titled "Regeneration and Renewal." Part B continues directly.]

Let me read to you 1 John chapter 2, verse 19, to show you the dangers of having only one of these two connections — external and internal — without the other. Reading from verse 18: "Children, it is the last hour. And as you have heard that Antichrist is coming, so now many Antichrists have come. Therefore we know that it is the last hour. They went out from us, but they were not of us. For if they had been of us, they would have continued with us. But they went out that it might be plain that they are not of us."

Now, what has this to do with what we are talking about? It has a lot to do with it. Antichrists are not coming from among unbelievers. The Antichrist and the antichrists who already exist were all of Christian origin. They went out from us — they were at one time associated with the Church, they had a definite external connection. But their internal connection with the Church was dangerously weak. And if that is your condition, not only may you one day fall away from grace, but worse than that, you may end up being one of the antichrists. Many of the people most hostile to the gospel are not unbelievers but people who once attended Christian schools, once were in Christian families, and once were so-called Christians. Because they know something about the Church, they are the most dangerous to it. Unbelievers are no danger to the Church; these who were once in it and then went out are.

You also notice in a church that those with weak internal connections to the Lord and to his Church are the people who become murmurers, grumblers, slanderers, and malcontents. Because of the weak internal connection, they live a defeated life. And because they live a defeated life, they breathe all kinds of dissatisfaction and take it out on the Church. The Church takes the brunt of their discontent. They become the troublemakers, the weak links, and finally, when they drop out altogether, they become the antichrists. So this problem is a very serious one, as John warns us. If you have a weak internal connection, if you are not truly abiding, not only will you not bear fruit — you could one day become one of these antichrists.

Now let us proceed quickly to understanding more about the internal relationship. We have understood at great length that the internal relationship is what the Lord wants to stress in this term abiding. But what does it consist of? What is the nature of this internal relationship? How do we communicate with the Lord? For clearly, to have an internal relationship means that we can communicate effectively with him. But how do we do this?

When you pray to the Lord, do you feel that your prayer is more or less one-way traffic? All the arrows point one way, and you wonder when anything comes back the other way. You pray and pray and it all seems to be a monologue. Your headphones seem to have gone out of operation — only the transmitter works but nothing comes back. And you wonder, what's the use of this kind of Christian life? It seems to be all one way. For this to work, there has to be two-way communication.

Now let us turn to verse 7 of John 15 and see how this two-way communication works: "If you abide in me and my words abide in you, ask whatever you will, and it shall be done for you." Several times it says "you abide in me and I in you," and suddenly here we find something different: "my words abide in you." This concept of the word of Jesus is vital to the whole matter of abiding. In verse 3 he already spoke about the word: "You are made clean by the word." Twice in these seven verses he has spoken about "my word."

What does it mean for a word to abide, to live in you? This only makes sense if the word is conceived of as something alive. Colossians 3:16 says: "Let the word of Christ dwell in you richly" — using precisely the Greek word katakaino, to inhabit, to live within you. And John 6:63: "My words, they are spirit and they are life." The words of Jesus are living things. I recently bought a theological book by a distinguished North American scholar, and in one reading I drained it — I exhausted its content, and a second reading wouldn't help me much. But when I come to John 15 — I have read it since I was a young Christian, and every time I read it, new depths of meaning come forth. You cannot do with the Bible what you do with an ordinary book. The scripture is something where understanding has nothing much to do with intellectual grasp but with spiritual vitality. It seems to grow bigger and bigger all the time.

Consider John 15:15: "No longer do I call you servants, for the servant does not know what his master is doing, but I call you friends, for all that I have heard from my Father I have made known to you." Do you understand the sentence intellectually? Probably yes. But do you understand it spiritually — do you actually know what it means that he has made known to you everything the Father has shown him? I think not, for most. That is why this word cannot be exhausted intellectually. It speaks of levels of spiritual content and experience that you do not understand until they become true for you. When Romans 6 says "you have died" — if you haven't died, you can read it and intellectually seem to understand it, but you don't understand it spiritually. When you have died, suddenly: now I know what Paul is talking about. In the same way, "abide in me" — do you know what it means? Not until you have this inner connection.

So how does the Lord speak to us in this internal relationship? He speaks first and foremost through the word that he has already taught. John 14:26: "But the Counselor, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will teach you all things and bring to your remembrance all that I have said to you." While you are praying, the Holy Spirit is communicating to you and bringing his word vitally to your mind. All that you need to know for your daily Christian life, he has already told you. But you can't remember what you never knew in the first place, so it is important to have studied the word — and then the Holy Spirit brings it to your remembrance. And "remembrance" in John's usage means not just refreshing your memory but bringing the significance and relevance of what he said to your present situation. What you didn't see before now suddenly comes alive to you.

Of course this is not the only way the Holy Spirit speaks to us, but it is the first step. You cannot go to the second step until you have grasped the first. The second step is when he speaks to you about things which are not a specific passage in scripture — when he tells you about this or that event. Like Agabus, who knew through the Holy Spirit that a famine was coming to Judea and was able to prepare the Church. Or the Apostle Paul, comforted directly by the Lord in prison. Or what happened to me this very morning: I woke up and the Lord was communicating with me, warning me that our time for the Church generally in the world is drawing rapidly to a close, that serious things are going to happen in the world very soon. The words "wars and rumors of wars" were before my eyes in a very vivid dream or vision. So much so that I found myself almost beginning to sweat at the sight of these things that were shown to me. The warning to me means: press on, your time is getting short, these peaceful circumstances we enjoy right now are not going to last all that much longer. This is very much like the warning God gave to Agabus about the famine — that the Church should prepare.

So learn to abide. First and foremost learn to listen to the Holy Spirit as he brings alive what the Lord Jesus has already said. If you don't hear what he has already said, if you are not listening to what applies to your present circumstances, then he has no use to tell you about things which are to come. As verse 13 says: "When the Spirit of truth comes, he will guide you into all truth. He will not speak on his own authority, but whatever he hears he will speak, and he will declare to you the things that are to come." Learn to abide. For he will not speak of his own authority, but whatever he hears he will speak." And notice these words: "And he will declare to you the things that are to come." That is exactly what the Lord did to me this very morning. Declare to me the things that are to come.

So then let us enter close into our walk with the living God. Learn to abide in him. Learn to have this two-way communication with the living God. And those who abide in him -- why did I stress this message today? What has it got to do with renewal and regeneration? What it has to do with renewal is this very thing: that those who abide in him do not sin. So long as you abide in him you do not sin. The moment you stop abiding in him you may well be into sin and deep into it too. Abide in him. Communicate with him in this internal relationship with him moment by moment. And you will find that you have neither the mind nor the inclination to sin. You cannot sin as you abide in him. And his power is constantly flowing into you and bringing forth much fruit. Let us be quiet before God in prayer.
