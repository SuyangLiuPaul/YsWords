# The Goal of Renewal: Fulfilling Christ's Saving Mission — Part 1 (1983-05-08)

After a break of two months, we continue in our series on regeneration and renewal. And today we are speaking particularly about renewal and the goal of renewal. In other words, if we are constantly being renewed, where are we being renewed? Into what kind of image are we being renewed into? Where are we going in this process of renewal?

We saw earlier, in some earlier messages, that perfection is an integral part of renewal. We can say, in fact, that it is the direction and goal of renewal. We are being renewed. Renewed into what? Well, into what we have described in scripture as perfection. In the words of scripture, perfection, as we saw, means not the eradication of sin. I am at pains to keep repeating this point. Not the elimination of sin, so that we become sinless in our person or in our nature. But perfection in scripture means the overwhelming power of God in our lives, through the Spirit, by which we are enabled to overcome sin. So perfection then, and I'm at pains to repeat this again and again, has nothing to do with a doctrine of eradication of sin from our lives, but the overcoming, the consistent overcoming of sin in our lives, by God's Spirit.

Now, if then perfection is triumphing over sin, what is the goal of this triumphing over sin? What is the direction in which this perfection is taking us? We can say very briefly that it is to become like Christ. It is to become like Him. But that is exactly what our subject is about today. What does it mean to become like Christ? What does being like Christ mean? Does it mean to be gentle Jesus, meek and mild, to be like that, to be kind, to be holy, to be nice, to be pleasant, to be all of these nice things, which indeed certainly includes all that? But is that then our goal, that the direction of renewal, our being born again, our regeneration, and the whole process of renewal is simply to make us like Jesus in this sense, to be meek and mild, to be humble, to be kind, to be nice, and all these good things? Or does even this have yet some -- can this be, even this be defined in terms of something more dynamic, if I can put it like this?

You'll say, what do you mean by more dynamic? Well, by this we mean, can all of these qualities be defined in terms of a mission, of a task in which we are to fulfill in this generation? In other words, are we just to become meek and mild because it's rather nice to be meek and mild, or holy, or pleasant, or whatever it is? Or is there something that these qualities are meant to accomplish in us, and for the well-being of others, and for the glory of God? If, in other words, the question is, is the goal of renewal, and the purpose of renewal, simply to make us individually nicer people, better people, and that's all there is to it? Or does our calling not merely mean that we should ourselves have a better character, but that it means that we have a task in these last days to fulfill? Does God just want to make you a rather nice person, that you can go about doing your own thing as a nice person, a godly person, a kind person, and all of these things are very fine and very good. But does that define our mission in these last days as disciples of Christ? That is the question we want to tackle.

Does becoming like Jesus merely mean that we are like him in what we are, or does it also mean that we become like him in what he does? That's to put the question in another way. Can it then be said that to be like Jesus is that we do what he does? That the task that God gave him to do is also now transferred to us to do. That renewal is not merely having as its purpose the fact that we become nicer and better people, which indeed it should be, and that is in order, but that is not enough. That the purpose of our transformation is that becoming like Jesus, we may fulfill his saving mission in the world. That is the kind of question we want to look at.

And I'm proposing to you today that the teaching of scripture is saying not merely that becoming like Christ is -- and when we sing this song, "O to be like thee, O to be like thee in your love and your kindness" and so forth, that is fine. But we have to go more than that. We have to be like thee in the taking up of our cross and following him. We have to be like thee. Even to the point of laying down our lives for his people, for the salvation of the world. Not, as I have emphasized in the past, that our death or our life can atone for the sins of others. This we cannot do. Only Jesus can do that. But that we are to take his atoning life and transmit it to others. And I want to show you as we go on in this study that if we don't live in this self-giving way, we don't become like Jesus. No matter how we might think ourselves to be meek and mild and gentle and humble and kind and all of these things, we have not become like Christ. We do not become like Christ until we live like him, that is, for the very same goal and mission for which he lives.

That is a tall order. But that is the order to which we have been called. And I would like that in this sharing today, in the exposition today, we may catch a vision of our time. The fact that we are to live for others and live sacrificially is something I have touched on more than once before and there is nothing new in this. But today I would like to be able more and more to kindle in our hearts through the word of God the vision of this task. For it strikes me ever more clearly that the people of God today do not have a vision. And when you don't have a vision, you don't know where you are going. And when you don't know where you are going, you don't even know why you are supposed to be gentle and meek and mild and loving and all these things. Why should I be any of these things?

So in summary, for this part of what we have covered so far, let me say this. The goal of renewal is perfection. Perfection is to become like Christ. Becoming like Christ is not merely a matter of being like Him in terms of character, though it includes that. But becoming like Christ in scripture means that we walk the way He walked. We do the work that He did. And we are to accomplish the task that He has entrusted to us. That is the exciting -- if I may use the word exciting, and I don't see why only worldly people are allowed to use words like exciting -- the exciting task. The exciting vision to which God has called us.
